# ML23 Instructor guide: Explain the project to a parent

Private. Keep this file out of the student folder.

**Objective.** By the end: you can explain your project in five minutes with five slides, one demonstration, one chart and one limit, without jargon.

**Question of the day.** Can a parent say what my model predicts, how I tested it and why it is limited?

Prerequisite: ML22 The final test and a reproducible handover.

## The 180 minutes

| Minutes | Activity |
|---|---|
| 10 | recall |
| 40 | five slides |
| 15 | break |
| 50 | rehearsals with a timer |
| 20 | optional modern-AI orientation or second rehearsal |
| 30 | individual feedback |
| 15 | save backups and exit check |

Where it overruns: The optional orientation is 20 minutes after the rehearsal. Skip it if any learner still needs rehearsal support.

## Block A. Five slides, five minutes

Build five slides: my question; what data I used; what I built; my result against the baseline; the limit and the next step. Say what a prediction, a feature and a target are in plain words.

Ask for the prediction first: Write on a card, in one sentence each, what a prediction, a feature and a target mean for your project.

Correct result: A listener should be able to say what was predicted, how it was tested and why the result is limited. One chart and one worked prediction say more than a list of library names. Show only approved, non-personal data. Prepare a screenshot backup and disclose any assistance. Publishing online is optional and needs agreement.

Investigate, with answers:

- What makes a slide too crowded? Answer: More than one idea, or code the audience cannot read from the back.
- What belongs on the limit slide? Answer: That the data is invented, the test is small, and the model is a demonstration.

Modify: Time yourself giving the five slides without looking at notes. Answer: Five minutes is enough for one question, one demonstration and one chart. Cut anything that takes more.

## Block B. Rehearse and receive feedback

Give the talk to a partner and answer one question. Your partner says back what the model predicts and how you tested it.

Ask for the prediction first: Predict the question a parent is most likely to ask.

Correct result: Common parent questions: where did this number come from, what did you leave out, and what cannot this model tell us. Answer by pointing to the chart or the table. If the partner cannot repeat your target and baseline, replace explanation with a worked example.

Investigate, with answers:

- If the explanation is mostly code, what should replace it? Answer: The question, a labelled chart and a worked prediction.
- Is it fine to say I do not know? Answer: Yes, and say what you would check next.

Modify: Say your project's limit in one sentence a younger student would follow. Answer: For example: The fares are invented, so this shows how a model works and not what real riders pay.

## Make: reference

A listener can say what was predicted, how it was tested and why the result is limited. The backup opens without the laptop.

Evidence to collect: Five slides saved as showcase.pptx, a PDF or screenshot backup, and a timed rehearsal with feedback.

## Misconceptions to listen for

- More detail makes a better talk.
- The code is the most important slide.
- A limitation weakens my project.

## Questions to ask, with expected answers

- What do parents need to hear first? Expected: What the model predicts, in everyday words.
- When can the modern-AI orientation be used? Expected: After the rehearsal, if every learner is ready. It replaces the optional second rehearsal and adds no assignment.

## Evidence and feedback

Record complete, needs repair or absent for each of the five learners, with one concrete repair action. Visit every learner, not only volunteers. Keep this guide private.
