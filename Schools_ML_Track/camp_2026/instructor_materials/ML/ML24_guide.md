# ML24 Instructor guide: Parent showcase and next steps

Private. Keep this file out of the student folder.

**Objective.** By the end: you can present your project, explain one decision, make one small code change without AI, and name your next learning goal.

**Question of the day.** Can I show and explain my own work with nothing to help but what I know?

Prerequisite: ML23 Explain the project to a parent.

## The 180 minutes

| Minutes | Activity |
|---|---|
| 30 | final setup and screenshot backup check |
| 10 | welcome |
| 40 | presentations and questions |
| 15 | break |
| 25 | unaided code variation for each learner |
| 20 | shared feedback and certificates |
| 40 | portfolio folder, reflection and next-step plan |

Where it overruns: For five learners the presentations fit in 40 minutes. A group of 15 needs a separate event slot.

## Block A. Present and explain

Each learner has five minutes to present and three minutes for questions. Explain the input, the output and one failure case.

Ask for the prediction first: Write the one decision in your project you will explain if asked.

Correct result: The instructor uses the same demonstration rubric for every learner. You show your own work, explain one decision, and answer an instructor-chosen question about your code unaided. Close assistant windows and turn off generative suggestions. If the technology fails, use the screenshot backup and explain the steps; a short live rerun can be arranged later.

Investigate, with answers:

- What is a failure case? Answer: An input where the model does badly, with a reason.
- What if the laptop fails? Answer: Show the backup and explain the workflow. Rerun later if evidence is missing.

Modify: Say one thing your model gets wrong and why. Answer: For example: it misses fares for very long trips, because it learned from short ones.

## Block B. A small change, unaided

The instructor chooses one small change to your code, such as a different test size or one more input. Make it and say what you expect.

Ask for the prediction first: Before the change, predict whether your error will go up or down.

Correct result: The change is small on purpose. It shows that you understand the notebook. A completion record follows the published policy: attendance, both checkpoints, the project and this demonstration. The certificate is issued by the programme provider and is not a university qualification or a certificate from another course provider.

Investigate, with answers:

- What does the certificate show? Answer: The track you finished and the evidence that was assessed.
- What is a good next learning goal? Answer: One specific thing, such as a second model on the same data, or a chart you did not finish.

Modify: Write your next learning goal on one line. Answer: A specific goal you can start next week.

## Make: reference

The instructor records the demonstration with the published rubric and keeps actual attendance apart from competence.

Evidence to collect: An individual demonstration, one unaided code change, feedback and a recorded completion decision.

## Misconceptions to listen for

- The certificate is a university qualification.
- I need AI to change my code.
- A mistake in the demonstration fails the course.

## Questions to ask, with expected answers

- What does the instructor check in the demonstration? Expected: Your own understanding of the work, through one decision and one small change.
- What happens if evidence is missing? Expected: The learner gets a short chance to supply it, following the published policy.

## Evidence and feedback

Record complete, needs repair or absent for each of the five learners, with one concrete repair action. Visit every learner, not only volunteers. Keep this guide private. Numeric results come from reference_values.json; explanations and live demonstrations need your judgement and are not auto-graded.
