# DA23 Instructor guide: Explain it to a parent

Private. Keep this file out of the student folder.

**Objective.** By the end: you can explain your project to a listener who has never seen code, in five minutes, with a chart and a limit.

**Question of the day.** Can a parent say what I found, how I know, and what the data cannot tell us?

**Where this is used.** Analysts explain their findings to busy people who do not write code. They say what they found, how they know, and what the data cannot tell. How would you say it to a parent in one minute?

Prerequisite: DA22 Report and handover.

## The 180 minutes

| Minutes | Activity |
|---|---|
| 15 | recall, then the five-slide plan |
| 45 | build the slides |
| 15 | break away from screens |
| 60 | timed rehearsals in turn with peer feedback |
| 30 | repair, backup and the optional modern AI orientation |
| 15 | exit ticket, then save |

Where it overruns: Five learners at five minutes each plus questions fill the rehearsal block. Rotate the listener role.

## Make: reference

Five slides, one idea each, no code on screen unless it answers a question. A listener can say what was compared, the unit, the biggest difference and the limitation. The speaker can point to the notebook cell behind each number. A screenshot backup exists.

Build log entry to look for: showcase.pptx and a PDF backup, a timed rehearsal and a feedback note from a peer.

## Misconceptions to listen for

- A good talk lists the libraries used.
- More slides mean a stronger talk.
- A live demonstration must always run.

## Questions to ask, with expected answers

- What would a parent say your project found? Expected: A plain sentence with one number and one comparison.
- What is your backup if the laptop fails? Expected: A PDF and screenshots, and a spoken explanation.

## Online version

For a hybrid group. The live class is 90 minutes on video with screen sharing. The learner finishes the build in their own time, about an hour. The full rules are in handbook/HYBRID_DELIVERY.md.

### Live clock, 90 minutes

| Minutes | Activity |
|---|---|
| 5 | Recall: three questions on paper, then say the answers aloud |
| 35 | Read the brief together on the shared screen, then the first build step. Each learner shares their screen in turn. |
| 5 | Break away from the screen |
| 35 | Learners keep building. The instructor visits each learner by screen share, about seven minutes each. |
| 10 | Close: say the exit sentence aloud, then the rest of the build task is set |

### After-class build task, about an hour

1. Make: Make five slides: my question; what data I used; what I did; my most useful finding with its chart; the limitation and next step. Save as showcase.pptx and keep a PDF and screenshots as a backup. Rehearse with a timer. Practise answering: where did this number come from, what did you clean, and what can this data not tell us. Disclose any help or AI use. Show only invented or approved non-personal data. The instructor may follow the rehearsal with MODERN_AI_ORIENTATION.md, a 20-minute optional introduction with no assignment.

When you finish, write two sentences: what you changed and how you checked it. Add a build log entry. Send a photo of your work or the notebook by the route the organiser sets, before the next class.
