# How classes work: in person and online

Some learners take part in a mix of in-person and online classes. This page explains the weekly pattern, what an online class looks like, what each learner needs, and how we keep every learner safe. The camp has no tests and no marks. Every class, in person or online, ends with something you built and saved.

## The weekly pattern

| Class | Where | How long |
|---|---|---|
| One core class a week, on Saturday | In person at The National ICT Innovation Hub, Nakawa | 3 hours |
| The other core classes of the week | Online on video, with screen sharing | About 90 minutes live, then about an hour of your own build time |
<!--ML-->| Foundation labs F01 to F04, in weeks 1 to 4 | Online on a fourth day each week (the organiser sets the day) | 2 hours live, as designed |
<!--/ML-->
The organiser confirms the exact days and times before the first class. Parents and guardians are told the class times in writing.

## An in-person class (3 hours)

The same shape every time, with the instructor in the room.

| Minutes | What happens |
|---|---|
| 10 | Recall: three questions on paper |
| 50 | Block A: predict, run, investigate, modify |
| 15 | Break away from screens |
| 50 | Block B: predict, run, investigate, modify |
| 40 | Make: your own task and your project journal |
| 15 | Exit sentence, save, and a check with each learner |

## An online class (about 90 minutes live, then your own build time)

The live part is on video with screen sharing. You do the guess, the run and the questions with the instructor. You finish the building in your own time.

| Minutes | What happens live |
|---|---|
| 5 | Recall: three questions, answered aloud |
| 35 | Block A: predict, run, investigate |
| 5 | Break away from the screen |
| 35 | Block B: predict, run, investigate |
| 10 | Close: you say your exit sentence, then the build task is set |

The Modify steps and the Make task move to the after-class build task. It takes about an hour. Each session's sheet and notebook already hold these steps, so you know what to do.

### Returning your build task

Before the next class, send a photo of your work or your notebook. The route the organiser sets is the only route to use. A file saved on your laptop has not been shared until you send it that way. Use your nickname or learner code in file names. Add a line to your build log and tick the stamp on your progress map.

If you are stuck for 30 minutes, stop. Save the error message, take a photo and bring it to the next class.

## Before your first online class

Check each item at least a day before. Ask the instructor if anything fails.

1. A laptop, with its charger plugged in. A phone is not enough for the live part.
2. Internet that holds a video call. Test it at the place and time you will use.
3. Headphones, so you can hear clearly and your family can work nearby.
4. The camp notebook installed and tested. Open the first notebook (<!--ML-->ML01<!--/ML--><!--DA-->DA01<!--/DA-->), run one cell and save it. Run preflight_stack.py once.
5. A backup if the connection drops: the PDF sheet for the class and the notebook are already on your laptop. If the call drops, keep working from the sheet, rejoin when you can, and send your work as usual.
6. A notebook and pen, because every guess is written on paper first.

## Staying safe online

Under-18s learn safely when everyone follows the same rules.

- A parent or guardian knows the class link and is told the class times.
- All messages stay in the agreed channel. No private messages outside it, in either direction.
- No recording without written consent from a parent or guardian. Nobody takes screenshots of other learners.
- Your camera is on for the live part, so the instructor can see your paper guesses and help you.
- Choose a calm space, ideally in a shared part of the home. You can leave the call at any time and tell your guardian.
- If something makes you uncomfortable, tell a parent or guardian and the organiser. Nobody gets into trouble for telling.

If an online class is not working for a learner, the instructor will invite them to the Saturday class and tell the parent kindly.

## For the instructor: keeping a teenager engaged on video

A teenager drifts when the screen is the only thing in the room. These habits keep the live part alive.

- Short turns. Nobody listens for more than two minutes. Ask a question, then stop.
- Predict before run. Every code cell starts with a written guess. Ask learners to hold the paper guess up to the camera, then run it.
- Share the screen often. In each block, ask each learner to share their screen and change something while the others watch.
- Change the activity about every 10 minutes: talk, guess, run, investigate, share, break. Never two long talks in a row.
- Use names. Ask by name, kindly, and give a few seconds. Offer the chat for a first answer to a shy learner.
- Say the exit sentence aloud, one learner at a time. It takes 10 minutes at most with five learners.
- End on something they built. If it is small, say so and name what is next.
- Keep the 5-minute break real. Camera off, away from the screen.

## For the instructor: when to say "come in person"

Invite a learner to the Saturday class, kindly and with their parent informed, when any of these happen.

- The first class. Session 1 works best in the room, because notebooks and the folders must open.
- Two online classes in a row where the learner could not run the notebook or return a build task.
- A laptop or software problem that cannot be fixed on screen.
- A weak or broken connection three times in a row.
- Any safeguarding concern.
- A project week or the showcase, when the learner needs help in person.

## For the instructor: online setup check

Do this once before the first online class and again whenever the platform changes.

1. The link, the time and these rules are sent to every parent or guardian in writing.
2. The organiser has confirmed the return route for build tasks, the platform and the consent form for recording.
3. Test screen sharing, sound and the paper-guess-to-camera habit with a learner.
4. Keep the PDF sheet and notebook for each class in a folder you can share or print at short notice.
5. Keep a list of which learners returned each build task. It shows who needs a Saturday visit.
6. Never use a private account or message to contact a learner.
