## Words for today

| Word | What it means | Think of it like this |
|---|---|---|
| merge key | A column that identifies a row and is shared by two tables, such as product_id. | The item number on both the price board and the receipt. |
| merge | Join two tables by matching their key columns. | Matching each receipt with its line on the price board. |
| left merge | Keep every row of the first table, even when nothing matches. | Keep every receipt, even for a missing price board line. |
| unique | Appears once only. A key must be unique in the table it looks up. | One item number per product. |
| fan-out | A merge that makes extra rows because a key appears twice, and inflates every total. | One receipt copied for every duplicated price line. |
| reconcile | Check that two routes to the same total agree, or explain why not. | Counting the cash tin and the receipts and comparing them. |

## Block A. A merge you can trust

sales.csv holds the product_id of each sale. products.csv says which category each product belongs to. Merge them on product_id. The row count must not change, because each sale matches exactly one product.

**Predict.** Predict the row count after the merge and which category earns the most. Write your guess on paper first.

```python
import pandas as pd
sales = pd.read_csv("sunrise_sales.csv")
products = pd.read_csv("sunrise_products.csv")
sales["line_value"] = sales["units"] * sales["unit_price_ugx"]
print(products["product_id"].is_unique)
merged = sales.merge(products[["product_id", "category"]], on="product_id", how="left")
print(len(sales), len(merged))
by_category = merged.groupby("category")["line_value"].sum().sort_values(ascending=False)
print(by_category)
print(by_category.sum())
```

**Run it, then compare.** The key is unique (True). Both tables have 1513 rows. The categories rank Household 2082700, Drinks 1863200, Stationery 1686500 and Snacks 743500, and they add to 6375900. The count and the total both match the true values, so the merge is safe.

**Investigate.**

1. Why check that product_id is unique before merging?
2. How do you know the merge did not lose or add rows?

## Block B. The fan-out trap

sunrise_products_practice.csv is a copy of the products table with one product listed twice. Merge with it and watch the totals. No error appears.

**Predict.** Predict what happens to the row count and the total revenue. Write your guess on paper first.

```python
practice = pd.read_csv("sunrise_products_practice.csv")
print(practice["product_id"].is_unique)
bad = sales.merge(practice[["product_id", "category"]], on="product_id", how="left")
print(len(bad), bad["line_value"].sum())
print(bad["line_value"].sum() - sales["line_value"].sum())
print(practice[practice.duplicated("product_id", keep=False)])
```

**Run it, then compare.** The key is not unique (False). The merged table has 1628 rows, which is 115 too many, and the total is 7079900, which is 704000 too high. The duplicate is Juice with product_id 6. Python raised no error. Only the check of the row count and total finds it.

**Investigate.**

1. Why did Python not warn us?
2. What two numbers give the problem away?
