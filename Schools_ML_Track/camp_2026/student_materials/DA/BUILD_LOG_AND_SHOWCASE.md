# Build log and showcase guide

The camp has no tests, no marks, no scores and no pass line. It is a holiday programme that gives you an extra skill. In every class you build something and save it. At the end you show a finished project to your family. That is the whole plan.

## The build log

The build log is a short record of what you made, one entry for every class.

- Keep it in a file called BUILD_LOG.md in your MyWork folder, or in the last cell of the class notebook.
- Each entry has four lines: the date and class (for example ML07), the file you saved, one sentence on what you made, and one sentence on what to try next. A fifth line is optional: "Project corner: one idea for my project".
- The saved notebook from the Make task is the main thing in each entry. If you only finished part of it, save that part and say so.
- Your instructor looks at the log with you, kindly, and does not score it.

Example entry:

> 12 December, DA09. Saved DA09.ipynb. I grouped the sales by branch and checked the total against my hand count. Next time I want to try a second chart.
> Project corner: I could group my football results by team in the same way.

## The progress map and badges

Each learner has My_Progress_Map.pdf, one A4 page. It has eight week rows. Each row has a badge name, the product of the week and three stamp circles (ML adds a small stamp for the lab in weeks 1 to 4). Colour in a stamp when you finish a class and show your build, or the instructor stamps it. Three stamps win the badge for the week. The finish line is the parent showcase. Badges are a game and a record of progress. They are not marks, and nobody is ranked.

The box at the bottom is for your project. In class 1 you write a topic you love. By week 4 you write a project question that grows from the topic, and in week 7 the question becomes the plan. Use the supplied data by default. Real public data is used only after you and the instructor have read its licence.

Online classes. A learner who takes a class online sends a photo of the build task, or the notebook, by the route the organiser sets. That counts for the stamp and for the build log entry. The rules for online classes are in HYBRID_DELIVERY.md.

## Milestone show-and-tells

A show-and-tell is a short, friendly turn to show the group something you built. Nobody is ranked and nobody is graded.

| When | What you show | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Class 12 (end of week 4) | Your first chart and your project question | 2 minutes |
| Class 18 (end of week 6) | Your fair comparison (ML) or your reconciled total (DA) | 2 minutes |
| Class 19 or 20 (week 7) | Your project plan and one early result | 2 minutes |

How it works. You show your screen or a printout and say what you made, how you checked it, and one thing you would try next. The listeners say one thing they liked and ask one question. If you prefer, you can show your work to the instructor alone. Offer other ways to show the same thing, for example a printed page or a recorded screen, for any learner who wants one.

## The final project

Every learner builds one project from the supplied data and keeps the files together in a project folder: project.ipynb, a data dictionary, a short report (report.pdf or report.docx), AI_USE_LOG.md, and for Data Analysis a cleaning_log.md. SHOWCASE_GUIDE.md describes what a great project shows. It is a guide for you and for the instructor's feedback. It is not a scoring sheet.

Project questions grow from the topic on the progress map. They are introduced in week 4, fixed in week 7, and the folder is saved together in class 22. Keep to skills taught in the camp and to approved data.

## The parent showcase

In the last class, parents and guardians join for the showcase.

- Each learner presents for about five minutes with five slides, then takes about three minutes of questions.
- After the talk the instructor suggests one small change to your code, for example a different filter or one more input. You make it live and say what you expect. It is a chance to show how your notebook works.
- Show your work with no AI assistant open. If the technology fails, use your PDF and screenshots and explain the steps. A short live rerun can be arranged afterwards.
- Do not display private information. Photographs are taken only where consent was given.

Plan for one group of five: 10 minutes welcome, 40 minutes presentations and questions, 10 minutes break, 20 minutes shared feedback, certificates and photos (where consented), which is 80 minutes. Use the remaining class time for setup and reflection. A combined event for 15 learners needs at least 120 minutes for talks and questions alone, plus welcome, breaks and certificates, so book a separate event slot.

## Feedback

Each learner receives one strength and one next step. Give them kindly and privately, in a short chat after a show-and-tell and after the showcase. Parents receive the same two things in a short note: what their learner built, one strength and one next step. Do not rank learners, compare them in public, or display private notes.

## The certificate

The programme certificate is for completing the programme. It goes to a learner who has done all three of these:

1. Attended, or made up, most classes. The working rule is at least 20 of the 24 core classes. ML learners also join the four foundation labs F01 to F04, in class or by make-up.
2. Kept the build log, with an entry for the classes attended.
3. Shown a finished project at the parent showcase.

There are no marks. A learner who has not yet done all three receives a participation record and an agreed chance to finish, such as a make-up class, a catch-up build task or a private show-and-tell. Confirm and publish these rules before enrolment.

Record certificates privately with learner name, track, completion date, issuer and certificate identifier. Use actual hours only if they were tracked. The certificate is a programme certificate. It is not a university qualification or a certificate from Google, IBM or another course provider.

## Making up a missed class

A learner who misses a class sees the class sheet, does the Make task at home or in a catch-up slot, saves it, adds a build log entry and shows it to the instructor for a short chat. That counts as made up.

## Responsible AI literacy

Read AI_USE_POLICY.md. Weeks 1 to 6: no AI-generated solutions for core exercises, labs or build tasks. Weeks 7 and 8: demonstration, critique and limited supervised use only after independent foundations. Keep AI_USE_LOG.md for any permitted help, and write "No AI assistance used" when there was none. You must understand, test and explain all of your own work. Your showcase is presented without an AI assistant open. Class 21 uses AI_CODING_ASSISTANTS_LAB.md within the existing practice time.
