# DA19 Project question and a safe plan

Week 7, Show it | Session 19 of 24 | Python for Data Analysis

**Today's question.** Which three questions can our data answer, and how will we show the answers?

**Objective.** By the end: you can write a narrow project plan with a question, the data, the measures, the charts and the limits, and have it approved.

Before this session: DA18 Checkpoint two: clean, merge and reconcile. If you cannot explain the last example, use its error card with the instructor first.

## Words for today

| Word | What it means | Think of it like this |
|---|---|---|
| project question | One question a table can answer with numbers. | Which item sells most, asked of a till roll. |
| scope | How much the project covers, and what it leaves out. | The fence round a garden. |
| data dictionary | A list of every column with its meaning and unit. | The key to a map. |
| limitation | What the data or method cannot tell us. | The part of the map that is blank. |
| licence | The rules that say how you may use a dataset. | The terms on a borrowed book. |
| disclosure | A plain statement of any help or AI tool you used. | Writing your sources at the end of an essay. |

## Block A. From a topic to three questions

A project serves a reader. Decide who needs the answer and what choice it could help with. Review AI_USE_POLICY.md now. Any permitted supervised AI help is written in AI_USE_LOG.md, and an unaided attempt always comes first. No forecasting or model training is required.

**Predict.** Predict which of these questions the Sunrise Shops data can answer with the taught methods: (a) Which category earns the most at each branch? (b) Why do people like Campus Gate? (c) What will sales be next month? Write your guess on paper first.

**Run it, then compare.** Question (a) can be answered: it needs the sales, products and branches tables, a merge and a group total. Question (b) asks for reasons, and the data has no opinions. Question (c) asks for a forecast, which this course does not teach. A narrow descriptive question with named columns is the right size.

**Investigate.**

1. What makes question (a) answerable?
2. How would you narrow question (b) so the data can answer it?

**Modify.** Write one more question the Sunrise data can answer, and name the columns it needs.

## Block B. The plan and the data

Your PROJECT_PLAN.md has seven headings: question, reader and use, data source and licence, measures and columns, method, charts, limitations. Start with Sunrise Shops, which is invented and needs no permission. You may use a public real dataset, for example from the Uganda Bureau of Statistics or the World Bank, once the licence has been read at the source, the instructor has approved it, and it holds no personal information.

**Predict.** Predict which heading is hardest to write, and why. Write your guess on paper first.

**Run it, then compare.** Most learners find limitations hardest, because it means saying what the data cannot show. A good limitation names a missing column or a kind of conclusion the project will not draw: no costs means no profit, invented data means no claim about real shops.

**Investigate.**

1. Why must the licence be read at the source?
2. What goes in the data dictionary?

**Modify.** Write the limitations heading for your own plan in two sentences.

## Error card

| You see | It means | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| The plan promises profit | The data has no costs, so profit cannot be worked out. | Change the measure to revenue and say so in the limitations. |
| A question needs a column that does not exist | The question asks for something the data never recorded. | Narrow the question to the columns you have, or choose another dataset. |
