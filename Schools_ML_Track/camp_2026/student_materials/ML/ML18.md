## Block A. A three-way split

First lock 20% of the flowers away as the final test, then take a quarter of what remains for validation. stratify keeps each species equally represented in every part.

**Predict.** Predict the three sizes. They should add up to 150. Write your guess on paper first.

```python
from sklearn.datasets import load_iris
from sklearn.model_selection import train_test_split
X, y = load_iris(return_X_y=True)
X_dev, X_test, y_dev, y_test = train_test_split(X, y, test_size=0.2, random_state=7, stratify=y)
X_train, X_val, y_train, y_val = train_test_split(X_dev, y_dev, test_size=0.25, random_state=7, stratify=y_dev)
print(len(X_train), len(X_val), len(X_test))
```

**Run it, then compare.** The cell prints 90, 30 and 30. stratify=y means each species has the same share in every part: 10 of each species in validation and in the final test. The final test is now locked and must not be used to make any choice.

**Investigate.**

1. Why are the three parts 60%, 20% and 20%?
2. What does stratify do that a plain shuffle does not?

## Block B. Choose on validation, test once

Compare a tree of depth 1 and one of depth 3 on validation only. Freeze the winner. Then run the final test once.

**Predict.** Predict which depth wins on validation. Write it down before you run. Write your guess on paper first.

```python
from sklearn.tree import DecisionTreeClassifier
from sklearn.dummy import DummyClassifier
from sklearn.metrics import accuracy_score, confusion_matrix
baseline = DummyClassifier(strategy="most_frequent").fit(X_train, y_train)
val_baseline = accuracy_score(y_val, baseline.predict(X_val))
tree1 = DecisionTreeClassifier(max_depth=1, random_state=7).fit(X_train, y_train)
tree3 = DecisionTreeClassifier(max_depth=3, random_state=7).fit(X_train, y_train)
score1 = accuracy_score(y_val, tree1.predict(X_val))
score3 = accuracy_score(y_val, tree3.predict(X_val))
if score3 > score1:
    chosen_depth = 3
    final_model = tree3
else:
    chosen_depth = 1
    final_model = tree1
final_acc = accuracy_score(y_test, final_model.predict(X_test))
print(val_baseline, score1, score3, chosen_depth, final_acc)
print(confusion_matrix(y_test, final_model.predict(X_test)))
```

**Run it, then compare.** The baseline scores exactly one third on validation, because each species is a third. The deeper tree usually wins on validation. The choice is made from the validation scores and then frozen. final_acc is the one honest number: it comes from 30 flowers the choice never saw. The report needs the split sizes, the baseline, both validation scores, the chosen depth, the final accuracy, the confusion matrix and two limitations.

**Investigate.**

1. Which scores did you use to choose the depth?
2. What is one limitation of a final test of 30 flowers?
