## Block A. Present and explain

Each learner has five minutes to present and three minutes for questions. Explain the input, the output and one failure case.

**Predict.** Write the one decision in your project you will explain if asked. Write your guess on paper first.

**Run it, then compare.** The instructor uses the same demonstration rubric for every learner. You show your own work, explain one decision, and answer an instructor-chosen question about your code unaided. Close assistant windows and turn off generative suggestions. If the technology fails, use the screenshot backup and explain the steps; a short live rerun can be arranged later.

**Investigate.**

1. What is a failure case?
2. What if the laptop fails?

**Modify.** Say one thing your model gets wrong and why.

## Block B. A small change, unaided

The instructor chooses one small change to your code, such as a different test size or one more input. Make it and say what you expect.

**Predict.** Before the change, predict whether your error will go up or down. Write your guess on paper first.

**Run it, then compare.** The change is small on purpose. It shows that you understand the notebook. A completion record follows the published policy: attendance, both checkpoints, the project and this demonstration. The certificate is issued by the programme provider and is not a university qualification or a certificate from another course provider.

**Investigate.**

1. What does the certificate show?
2. What is a good next learning goal?

**Modify.** Write your next learning goal on one line.

## Error card

| You see | It means | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| The change breaks the notebook | A name or a cell order changed. | Restart, run all cells, then read the last line of the error. |
| The demonstration is too long | More than one idea per slide. | Use the five slides and leave the extra detail for questions. |
