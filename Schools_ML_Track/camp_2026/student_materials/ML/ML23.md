# ML23 Explain the project to a parent

Week 8, Hand over | Session 23 of 24 | Machine Learning

**Today's question.** Can a parent say what my model predicts, how I tested it and why it is limited?

**Objective.** By the end: you can explain your project in five minutes with five slides, one demonstration, one chart and one limit, without jargon.

Before this session: ML22 The final test and a reproducible handover. If you cannot explain the last example, use its error card with the instructor first.

## Words for today

| Word | What it means | Think of it like this |
|---|---|---|
| claim | A statement that needs a visible result. | I say the model predicts fares to within about UGX 3000. |
| demonstration | Showing your own work running or explaining its steps. | Showing the dish you cooked. |
| audience | The people listening and what they already know. | The parents in the room. |
| backup | A screenshot or saved file you can show if the laptop fails. | A spare key. |
| disclosure | Saying where help or AI was used. | Thanking those who helped. |

## Block A. Five slides, five minutes

Build five slides: my question; what data I used; what I built; my result against the baseline; the limit and the next step. Say what a prediction, a feature and a target are in plain words.

**Predict.** Write on a card, in one sentence each, what a prediction, a feature and a target mean for your project. Write your guess on paper first.

**Run it, then compare.** A listener should be able to say what was predicted, how it was tested and why the result is limited. One chart and one worked prediction say more than a list of library names. Show only approved, non-personal data. Prepare a screenshot backup and disclose any assistance. Publishing online is optional and needs agreement.

**Investigate.**

1. What makes a slide too crowded?
2. What belongs on the limit slide?

**Modify.** Time yourself giving the five slides without looking at notes.

## Block B. Rehearse and receive feedback

Give the talk to a partner and answer one question. Your partner says back what the model predicts and how you tested it.

**Predict.** Predict the question a parent is most likely to ask. Write your guess on paper first.

**Run it, then compare.** Common parent questions: where did this number come from, what did you leave out, and what cannot this model tell us. Answer by pointing to the chart or the table. If the partner cannot repeat your target and baseline, replace explanation with a worked example.

**Investigate.**

1. If the explanation is mostly code, what should replace it?
2. Is it fine to say I do not know?

**Modify.** Say your project's limit in one sentence a younger student would follow.

## Error card

| You see | It means | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| The slides run over five minutes | There are more than five ideas. | Keep one idea per slide and cut the rest. |
| The demonstration does not run | The notebook was not restarted or the data file was moved. | Show the screenshot backup and explain the steps. |
