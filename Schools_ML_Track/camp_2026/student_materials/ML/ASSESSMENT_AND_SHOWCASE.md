# Assessment and showcase guide

Proposed completion: at least 20 of 24 core meetings attended or made up; ML also completes F01–F04 evidence; both checkpoints, final project and demonstration completed; overall mark at least 50/100. Confirm and publish this policy before enrolment. Each mark must point to observable work. Skills checks support learning; this is not a professional licensing assessment.

## Six weekly practice portfolios

Weeks 1 to 6, each out of 10: correct required output 4; saved/reproducible method 3; explanation in the learner's own words 2; evidence of checking or repair 1. Average the six scores and multiply by 2 for the 20% course contribution. Do not count attendance as a technical skill mark. For ML weeks 1–4, include the foundation lab evidence in the same weekly portfolio; do not double count its score.

## Checkpoint one

Raw scale 20, weighted 15%: correct file/table setup 3; valid calculations 5; appropriate cleaning decisions and preserved raw data 4; labelled chart 4; findings and limitation 4. ML submits ML12.ipynb; DA submits DA12.ipynb plus short DOCX/PDF explanation. No penalty for honestly reporting that the supplied clean file did not need alteration.

## Checkpoint two

ML raw scale 20, weighted 20%: correct train/validation/test separation 5; baseline and candidate models fitted only on training 4; validation comparison and frozen choice 4; correct final metric/matrix 4; explanation and limitations 3. Use ML18 instructions and instructor reference. Do not grade the learner by a minimum accuracy threshold.

DA raw scale 20, weighted 20%: filtering/grouping SQL 5; valid join and key checks 4; null/count checks 3; agreement between SQL and pandas totals 4; saved query and explanation 4. Use DA18 and reference_values.json. Collect DA18.ipynb, DA18.sql and results.csv. Result screenshots without the query do not earn reproducibility marks.

## Final project

Raw scale 30, weighted 30% for each track: clear question and data origin 4; reproducible preparation 6; correct analysis/model workflow 8; checks and honest limitations 6; complete readable handover 6. ML requires a baseline and a final held-out test. DA requires an executable Python/pandas notebook with labelled charts, saved SQL and a short report. Restrict projects to taught skills and approved data. Project requirements are introduced in Week 4, fixed in Week 7 and submitted in Session 22.

Detailed scoring anchors are in PROJECT_RUBRIC.md. Collect AI_USE_LOG.md with the final hand-in; learners who used no assistance state that explicitly.

## Individual demonstration

Raw scale 15, weighted 15%: question and audience clarity 3; working demonstration or verified backup 4; explanation of one calculation/model decision and a small unaided modification 4; limitation and answers to questions 4. Use five minutes speaking and three minutes questions per learner. Do not grade confidence, accent or decorative slide design as technical mastery. Offer accessible ways of demonstrating the same evidence.

For one group of five: 10 minutes welcome, 40 minutes presentations/questions, 10 minutes break, 20 minutes shared feedback/certificates/photos only where consented = 80 minutes. Use remaining class time for setup and reflection. A combined 15-person event requires at least 120 minutes for talks/questions alone, plus welcome, breaks and certificates; book a separate realistic event slot rather than squeezing it into the five-person plan.

Parents receive a short explanation of what the learner built, one strength and one next step. Do not publicly rank students or display private marks. Record certificates privately with learner name, track, completion date, issuer and certificate identifier; use actual hours only if tracked.

## Responsible AI literacy

Read AI_USE_POLICY.md. Weeks 1–6: no AI-generated solutions for core exercises, labs or assessments. Weeks 7–8: demonstration, critique and limited supervised use only after independent foundations; keep AI_USE_LOG.md for any permitted assistance. Students must understand, test and explain all work. Final demonstrations and assessment explanations are completed without AI assistance. Core exercises remain unaided throughout. Session 21 uses AI_CODING_ASSISTANTS_LAB.md within the existing practice time.
