# Review of the Busoga Oils supply agreement against the NVF playbook (v2)

Three clauses break the playbook: clauses 3, 8 and 10. The other clauses meet it.

## Clauses that break the playbook

### Clause 3 (Price and price adjustment) breaks P3

- Quote: "The Supplier may change any price at its sole discretion by telling NVF in writing, and the new price applies to every order not yet delivered, including orders NVF has already confirmed."
- Rule broken: P3. NVF accepts a price change only with at least 30 days' written notice, a cap of 5% in any 12 months, and no effect on confirmed orders. It does not accept changes at the supplier's sole discretion.
- Why it matters: The clause breaks all four parts of P3.
  - It lets the Supplier set any price at its sole discretion.
  - It has no minimum notice period.
  - It has no cap.
  - It reprices orders NVF has already confirmed. NVF may have already committed to customers or budgeted at the old price.
- Change to ask for: Replace the sentence with wording like this. "The Supplier may change a price only by giving NVF at least 30 days' written notice. Price increases may not exceed 5% in total in any 12-month period. A new price does not apply to any order NVF has already confirmed."

### Clause 8 (Term and automatic renewal) breaks P6

- Quote: "unless one party gives the other written notice of non-renewal at least 90 days before the end of the current period."
- Rule broken: P6. Automatic renewal for 12 months or less is acceptable only if the notice period to stop renewal is no more than 30 days. The playbook does not accept a longer notice period.
- Why it matters: The 12-month term and 12-month renewals are acceptable. The 90-day notice period is three times the limit. NVF would have to decide about leaving 3 months early. If it misses the window, it is locked in for another full year.
- Change to ask for: Change "at least 90 days" to "at least 30 days". Keep the 12-month term and renewal periods.

### Clause 10 (Liability) breaks P8

- Quote: "limited to the fees NVF paid in the 3 months before the claim arose. This limit applies to every kind of claim, including claims about contaminated or unsafe goods."
- Rule broken: P8. The cap must be at least 12 months of fees. Claims about unsafe or contaminated goods, fraud and breach of data rules must have no cap.
- Why it matters: This breaks P8 in two ways.
  1. The cap covers only 3 months of fees, a quarter of the 12-month minimum.
  2. The clause expressly applies the cap to contaminated and unsafe goods. For a food business this is the biggest risk, because a contamination claim could far exceed 3 months of fees. The clause also applies the cap to fraud and data-rule breaches, which must be uncapped.
- Change to ask for: Rewrite the clause in two parts.
  - Raise the cap to "the fees paid or payable in the 12 months before the claim arose".
  - Add: "Nothing in this agreement limits or excludes the Supplier's liability for contaminated or unsafe goods, for fraud, or for breach of data protection obligations (clause 11)."
  - Delete the sentence "This limit applies to every kind of claim, including claims about contaminated or unsafe goods."

## Clauses checked that meet the playbook

- Clause 5 and P1: Payment is within 30 days of receiving a correct invoice. This meets P1.
- Clause 6 and P2: Interest is 1% a month, below the 1.5% limit. It applies to undisputed amounts only and applies equally to the Supplier's delayed refunds and credits. This meets P2.
- Clause 4 and P4: Delivery is within 3 working days of confirmation, which is inside the 5-day limit. Risk passes on signed delivery. This meets P4.
- Clause 7 and P5: NVF has 7 days to reject, and the Supplier replaces or credits within 14 days. This meets P5 exactly.
- Clause 9 and P7: A material breach not fixed within 14 days allows termination, and either party may terminate for insolvency. This meets P7.
- Clause 11 and P9: Confidentiality lasts 3 years after the end. The Supplier uses personal data only on NVF's written instructions, follows the Data Protection and Privacy Act 2019 and reports leaks within 72 hours. This meets P9. Clause 10 currently caps data-rule breaches, which conflicts with P8. That is fixed under clause 10 above.
- Clause 12 and P10: The law and courts of Uganda apply. This meets P10.
- Clause 2 (Goods) and clause 1 (Parties): No playbook rule applies.
