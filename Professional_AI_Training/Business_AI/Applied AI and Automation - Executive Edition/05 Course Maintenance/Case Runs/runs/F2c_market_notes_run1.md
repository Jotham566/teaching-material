**Briefing for the Sales Director: Maize flour price, Eastern Uganda, week 41**

Note: all four source notes are marked as invented training material, so none of these figures are real statistics.

**Bottom line**

The sources disagree on the price, so I cannot give you one reliable number. Both say the price rose slightly in week 41. They differ by UGX 500 per kg.

**The two price figures**

| Source | Week 41 average wholesale price | Change |
|---|---|---|
| Note 1, Savanna Market Watch bulletin | UGX 3,400 per kg | About 2% above week 40 (implies roughly UGX 3,330 in week 40) |
| Note 3, Lake Region Grain Desk price sheet | UGX 3,900 per kg | Up from UGX 3,800 in week 40 (about 2.6%) |

- The gap is about 15% of the lower figure. It is too large to explain by rounding.
- Note 3 says it rests on a small sample of 12 traders. Note 1 does not say how it was built. Neither source explains how it collected its prices, so I cannot tell which is more accurate. I have not picked one.
- Notes 2 and 4 give no maize flour price.

**What the sources agree on**

- The price is edging up, by roughly 2% to 3% on the week.
- Road delays around Mbale after the heavy rains are a cause. Note 1 lists them as something to watch, and Note 3 says traders blame them for the rise over the last two weeks.
- Note 4 says maize flour is not affected by the oil and shipping costs it covers.

**Supply and demand context (Note 1, with support from Note 2)**

- Supply from the new harvest is arriving slowly. Millers are running at about 70% of capacity. Note 1 calls supply the main risk for maize flour.
- Demand is steady from schools and small shops. Large buyers are waiting for lower prices in November.
- Shop owners in Eastern Uganda reported maize flour and rice sales up about 8% in October compared with September, helped by school feeding orders (Note 2). This is a shop-owner report and not a measured figure.
- Shops are buying smaller quantities more often, and say reliable delivery days matter as much as price (Note 2).

**What this means for sales**

- Plan for a stable to slightly rising price in the short term. The sources do not say where it goes after week 41. Large buyers expect a fall in November, but no source forecasts one.
- Reliable delivery matters as much as price to shop buyers, so Mbale road delays are a service risk as well as a price risk.

**Before you quote a price to customers**

1. Check the week 41 maize flour price against a source we trust, or ask our own buyers or field team what they paid. The two notes cannot both be right.
2. Do not use Note 1 and Note 3 together in one table, because they are not on the same basis.

**Out of scope**

Cooking oil (about UGX 5,150 to 5,200 per litre, with a possible rise of up to 5% after November) and the December demand outlook appear in the notes but do not concern the maize flour question, so I left them out.
