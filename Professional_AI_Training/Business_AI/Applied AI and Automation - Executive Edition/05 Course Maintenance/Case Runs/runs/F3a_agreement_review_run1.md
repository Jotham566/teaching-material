# Review of the Busoga Oils supply agreement against the NVF playbook (v2)

Three clauses break the playbook: clause 3, clause 8 and clause 10. The other nine clauses meet it.

## Clauses that break the playbook

### 1. Clause 3 (Price and price adjustment) breaks P3

- **Quote:** "The Supplier may change any price at its sole discretion by telling NVF in writing, and the new price applies to every order not yet delivered, including orders NVF has already confirmed."
- **Rule broken:** P3. NVF accepts a price change only with at least 30 days' written notice, a cap of 5% in any 12 months, and no effect on orders already confirmed. It does not accept changes at the supplier's sole discretion.
- **Why it matters:** The clause breaks all four parts of P3.
  - It is at the supplier's sole discretion.
  - It has no notice period, so a price can rise the moment NVF is told.
  - It has no cap, so the increase can be any size.
  - It reaches orders NVF has already confirmed. NVF may already have committed its own selling prices, so it could be forced to buy at a higher cost with no way to recover the difference.
- **Change to ask for:** Replace the sentence with wording like this: "The Supplier may change prices only by giving NVF at least 30 days' written notice. Price increases may not exceed 5% in total in any 12-month period. A new price does not apply to any order NVF has confirmed before the notice period ends." Delete "sole discretion" and "including orders NVF has already confirmed".

### 2. Clause 8 (Term and automatic renewal) breaks P6

- **Quote:** "...unless one party gives the other written notice of non-renewal at least 90 days before the end of the current period."
- **Rule broken:** P6. Automatic renewal is acceptable for periods of 12 months or less, but only if the notice period to stop renewal is no more than 30 days. NVF does not accept a longer notice period.
- **Why it matters:** The 12-month initial term and the 12-month renewal periods are fine. The 90-day notice period is three times the allowed maximum.
  - NVF has to decide about leaving three months before the term ends, which is long before it knows how the supplier has performed.
  - If NVF misses the date, it is locked in for another 12 months.
- **Change to ask for:** Change "90 days" to "30 days". Keep the 12-month renewal periods. Optionally, ask for a reminder from the Supplier 60 days before the deadline, so a missed date does not renew the contract by accident.

### 3. Clause 10 (Liability) breaks P8, in two ways

- **Quote A (the size of the cap):** "...is limited to the fees NVF paid in the 3 months before the claim arose."
- **Quote B (no carve-outs):** "This limit applies to every kind of claim, including claims about contaminated or unsafe goods."
- **Rule broken:** P8. A cap is acceptable only if it is at least 12 months of fees. Claims about unsafe or contaminated goods, fraud and breach of data rules must have no cap.
- **Why it matters:**
  - A 3-month cap is a quarter of the minimum. Early in the contract, or in a slow period, the cap could be very small.
  - The clause expressly caps contaminated or unsafe cooking oil. That is the biggest risk in a food supply: a recall, injury to consumers, or damage to NVF's brand could cost far more than 3 months of fees.
  - The clause says the cap applies to "any cause", so it also covers fraud and breach of data rules (clause 11), which the playbook says must be uncapped.
- **Change to ask for:** Two changes.
  - Raise the cap to at least the fees paid or payable in the 12 months before the claim. Where less than 12 months have passed, use 12 months of the expected fees.
  - Add a carve-out: "Nothing in this agreement limits or excludes the Supplier's liability for (a) unsafe or contaminated goods, (b) fraud, or (c) breach of clause 11 (personal data) or the Data Protection and Privacy Act 2019." Delete the second sentence of clause 10 ("This limit applies to every kind of claim, including claims about contaminated or unsafe goods"). Also change "for any cause" so it is subject to the carve-out.

## Clauses checked that meet the playbook (no change needed)

| Clause | Rule | Why it meets it |
|---|---|---|
| 4 Ordering and delivery | P4 | Delivery within 3 working days (limit is 5). Risk passes on signed delivery. |
| 5 Payment | P1 | Payment within 30 days of receiving a correct invoice. |
| 6 Late payment interest | P2 | 1% a month (limit 1.5%), undisputed amounts only, and the same rate applies to the Supplier's delayed refunds and credits. |
| 7 Quality and returns | P5 | 7 days to reject and 14 days to replace or credit. Both are exactly at the minimum, with no room to spare. |
| 9 Termination | P7 | Each party may end the agreement for an unfixed material breach (14 days) and for insolvency. |
| 11 Confidentiality and personal data | P9 | 3 years after the end, use only on NVF's written instructions, compliance with the Data Protection and Privacy Act 2019, and notice within 72 hours. |
| 12 Governing law | P10 | The laws and courts of Uganda. |

Clauses 1 and 2 are not covered by any playbook rule.

## Notes for the manager

- Clause 10 is the most serious problem, because it caps liability for contaminated oil. Clause 3 is the second most serious, because it can change the price of orders NVF has already confirmed. Clause 8 is the easiest to fix, since it is a single number.
- The clause 7 and clause 9 periods meet the playbook exactly at its limits. Do not accept any weakening of them in negotiation.
- Schedule 1 (the price list) was not provided, so I could not check it. Check it before signing, because clause 3 refers to it.
- The playbook says the Managing Director signs every supplier agreement. The signature block names Sarah Namukasa, Managing Director, so that is in order.
- Do not sign until clauses 3, 8 and 10 are changed.
