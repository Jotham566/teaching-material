# Dispatch Clerk, Jinja Depot: ranking of eight CVs

Everything below comes from the CVs as written. Nothing has been checked yet. The essential test is a relevant certificate or diploma, or at least 2 years in a store, depot or delivery role. The other essentials are good English plus Luganda or another local language, accuracy with numbers, and Google Sheets.

## Ranking for interview (strongest to weakest)

**1. Tumusiime Brian**
He has 7 years in food distribution, now as Dispatch Coordinator running 12 trucks and about 150 drops a week. He also holds a Diploma in Logistics and Supply Chain Management, a forklift licence, MoMo and Airtel reconciliation, Google Sheets and Luganda, so he meets every essential and the useful items. He already lives in Jinja, and his loading check against the order sheet matches duty 2 directly.

Verify before interview:
- Call the referee, Mr Byaruhanga, to confirm the dates, the title, and the figures (12 trucks, 150 deliveries a week, wrong items cut from 6 to 1 a week).
- Check the diploma with the institute.
- See the forklift licence itself.
- Ask why he is leaving a coordinator job for a clerk role. Check salary expectations and that he is not overqualified or likely to leave soon.
- The CV says he is "currently living in Jinja" while his job is in Mbarara. Clarify his current employment status and notice period.

**2. Avako Moses**
He has 2 years as a Delivery Clerk preparing delivery notes for 3 trucks and recording cash and mobile money on delivery. He sends the evening summary to the accountant, and he has a diploma in Accounting and Finance, Google Sheets and Luganda. This is nearly the exact job at smaller scale, and accuracy with numbers is likely to be strong.

Verify before interview:
- Check the diploma (2022) and confirm the delivery clerk dates and duties with Mrs Draru.
- He sits right at the 2-year minimum for delivery work, so confirm the exact start date.
- He lives in Tororo, so ask about relocation or commuting to Jinja, and whether he can do the 2 pm to 10 pm shift.
- Ask about his Google Sheets level ("basic formulas") with a short practical test.

**3. Wandera Isaac**
He has about 3 years coordinating about 40 deliveries a day across 15 boda riders and collecting cash on delivery, plus a Certificate in Procurement and Stores Management. That is real delivery and cash experience with route planning, and he speaks Luganda. He is weaker on paperwork (delivery notes, invoices, signed proof of delivery) and on structured Google Sheets use, since he works mostly through WhatsApp groups.

Verify before interview:
- Confirm his role and the volumes with Ms Nambozo.
- Ask how cash is recorded and reconciled and whether any shortages happened.
- Check the certificate.
- He lives in Mbale, so ask about relocating to Jinja (the job is based in Jinja with only some travel to Mbale).
- Test his Sheets skills.

**4. Atim Beatrice**
She has about 4 years in stores and logistics, including preparing truck delivery documents at a cargo company, and she already lives in Jinja. She speaks Luganda and Luo and uses Google Sheets. Her experience meets the essential on its own, so the unconfirmed Kenyan diploma is not a barrier, and she was honest to flag it.

Verify before interview:
- Confirm the Kisumu job and dates with Mr Otieno (cross-border, so reference checks may be slower).
- Check the diploma with the issuing institute. Ugandan equivalence is not needed to meet the essential because the experience route applies.
- Check that she has the right to work in Uganda (she appears to be Ugandan, but confirm).
- Her current role is stores and not delivery, so ask about mobile money and driver or route work.

**5. Apio Lillian**
She has about 3 years as a Stores Clerk (receiving, issuing against signed requests, a weekly reconciliation) and earlier cash-up and mobile money receipts in sales, plus a Certificate in Business Administration. She meets the essential. The gaps are that she has no dispatch or delivery work, her Sheets skill is "basic", her Luganda is only "some", and she lives in Gulu. The 2021 to 2023 career break for family care is legitimate and should not count against her.

Verify before interview:
- Confirm the job with Mr Ocaya.
- Check the certificate.
- Confirm she will actually relocate, and when.
- Test Sheets and her spoken Luganda (Luo and English may suffice under "another local language", but the depot works across Busoga and Bugisu, so ask).

**6. Lomuria Samuel**
He has 5 years of practical experience, including loading trucks and checking each load against the delivery note, which is duty 2. He meets the experience route without a formal certificate. The main weaknesses are that he is only "learning" Google Sheets (an essential), and that he has not prepared invoices or recorded payments. He lives in Mbale.

Verify before interview:
- Confirm the Moroto and Mbale work history and dates with Mr Wamimbi.
- Test Sheets and arithmetic with a practical task.
- Ask about his experience with mobile money and cash.
- Confirm relocation.
- Treat him as a possible hire if Sheets skills are trainable, since his load checking is the closest match to depot work apart from Brian.

## Not shortlisted

**Mugabi Dennis: do not shortlist.** The CV is not credible.
- It claims "Operations Director" of a 200-truck, 150-staff fleet across East Africa, with a 400% profit increase, from 2024. In the same period he was a student, and his only qualification is a 2024 Diploma in Business Studies.
- It claims "over 10 years" of logistics experience but lists none before 2022. The employer is "Mugabi and Sons Trading" (apparently a family firm), there are no referees, and the language is vague.
- If the team wants to be sure, a short phone check with the employer would settle it. If the claims are false, treat this as a misrepresentation concern for HR.

**Nakato Prossy: do not shortlist for now (reserve).**
- She has a Bachelor of Business Administration, so on paper she meets the qualification test. Her depot and delivery experience is only a 3-month internship and weekend shop work.
- She is in Kampala. Her Sheets and customer-call skills fit the data entry and late-delivery messaging parts, but this role is physical, depot-based and shift-based with cash handling. Keep her on file in case the stronger candidates fall away or a junior office role opens.

## Notes for the whole shortlist
- Ask all candidates to bring original certificates, and ask for the one-page cover letter the advert requires. None of the CVs mention one, so check it was sent.
- Use the same short practical task for everyone: build a loading list from a sample order sheet in Google Sheets, then reconcile a small set of MoMo, Airtel and cash payments.
- Confirm every candidate can work both the 6 am and 2 pm shifts and occasionally travel to Mbale.
- Do not rank on gender, region of origin or career breaks. The ranking above rests only on the job-related evidence in the CVs.
- Referees are named by the candidates themselves, so ask for a second, independent contact where possible.

Suggested interview slate: candidates 1 to 5 (and 6 if time allows).
