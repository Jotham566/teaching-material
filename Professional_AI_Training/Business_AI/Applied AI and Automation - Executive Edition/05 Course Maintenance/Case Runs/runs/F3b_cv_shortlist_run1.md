# Dispatch Clerk, Jinja Depot: interview ranking

Note: all eight CVs are self-reported. Nothing below has been checked with referees or employers.

## Ranking, strongest to weakest

**1. Tumusiime Brian**
He has a diploma in logistics and 7 years in food distribution, including running dispatch for 12 trucks and about 150 deliveries a week. He already lives in Jinja, and he has forklift, MoMo/Airtel reconciliation, Google Sheets and Luganda, so he meets every essential and useful criterion.

**2. Avako Moses**
He has 2 years as a delivery clerk preparing delivery notes and recording cash and mobile money on delivery, plus an accounting diploma and Luganda. This is very close to the real duties, but he is based in Tororo (relocation needed) and has no forklift or route-planning experience.

**3. Atim Beatrice**
She has a supply chain diploma, 4 years of stores and logistics document work, and she lives in Jinja and speaks Luganda. The diploma is Kenyan and not yet confirmed as equivalent, but her work experience alone (over 2 years) meets the essential requirement.

**4. Wandera Isaac**
He has 3 years coordinating about 40 deliveries a day across 15 boda riders, including cash on delivery and route planning, with a stores certificate and Luganda. He lacks truck and paperwork experience (delivery notes, invoices), lives in Mbale, and his "Delivery Coordinator" role seems informal, so check the scale.

**5. Lomuria Samuel**
He has 5 years of practical store and loading work and checks loads against delivery notes, which is core to duty 2. He has no relevant qualification, only "learning" Google Sheets, and no Luganda listed (he does list Lumasaaba and Swahili, which may count as another local language), so he is a reserve for interview.

**6. Apio Lillian**
She has 2 years as a stores clerk (2023 to present), plus earlier cash and mobile money work, so she just meets the 2-year experience route. She has only basic Sheets and no delivery or dispatch experience, and she is in Gulu. The 2021 to 2023 career break should not count against her.

**7. Nakato Prossy**
She has a good degree and strong Sheets skills, but only a 3-month internship and a part-time weekend job, so she does not meet the 2-year experience route. The degree is "business", which does satisfy the qualification route, so she is technically eligible; she is a weak fit for a physical depot role.

## Not shortlisted

**Mugabi Dennis (8th): do not shortlist.** His CV is internally inconsistent. He claims 10+ years of experience, but his education history shows a UCE in 2021 and a diploma in 2024, and he lists 2022 to 2024 as "Student". He claims to be Operations Director of a 200-truck fleet and 150 staff since 2024 at a family-named firm, with a 400% profit increase. These claims are implausible and unsupported, no referees are named, and the profile is generic. Unless a referee check can substantiate any of it, he should not go forward.

**Nakato Prossy (7th)** is also a likely no. She is eligible only through the degree, her experience is too thin for a dispatch role handling cash and trucks, and she should be told about future graduate or trainee openings.

**Lomuria Samuel and Apio Lillian** (5th and 6th) are borderline. I would interview only the top 4 or 5 and keep them in reserve if the top candidates fall through.

Suggested shortlist: Brian, Moses, Beatrice, Isaac; Samuel as a reserve.

## Fairness notes

Rank on the job requirements only. Do not use home area, tribe or region, gender, or the career break as selection criteria. Candidates who must relocate should be asked, not assumed to be unwilling. Keep the same questions and score sheet for everyone.
