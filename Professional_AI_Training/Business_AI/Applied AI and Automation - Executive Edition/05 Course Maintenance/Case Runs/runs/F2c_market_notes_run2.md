**Briefing for the Sales Director: maize flour price, Eastern Uganda, week 41**

(All four notes are labelled fictional training documents. The figures below are from those notes only and are not real statistics.)

**Bottom line**
Maize flour wholesale prices rose slightly in week 41, but the notes disagree on the level. Note 1 says UGX 3,400 per kg and Note 3 says UGX 3,900 per kg, a gap of UGX 500. I cannot tell you which is right, so I would not give a single price until it is checked.

**What the notes say**
- Note 1 (Savanna Market Watch bulletin): UGX 3,400 per kg, about 2% above week 40.
- Note 3 (Lake Region Grain Desk price sheet): UGX 3,900 per kg, up from UGX 3,800 in week 40 (about 2.6%). It is based on only 12 traders.
- Both notes agree on the direction: a small rise of roughly 2% to 3% on the week.

**Why prices are rising**
- Supply: the new harvest is arriving slowly, and millers are running at about 70% of capacity (Note 1).
- Roads: Mbale traders say road delays over the last two weeks pushed the price up (Note 3). Note 1 also lists road conditions around Mbale as a risk, since trucks can be delayed by one or two days.
- Demand: shop owners report maize flour and rice sales up about 8% in October versus September, helped by school feeding orders (Note 2). Large buyers are holding off, waiting for lower prices in November (Note 1).

**What this means for sales**
- Expect prices to stay firm or edge up while supply is slow. Note 1 names supply as the main risk for maize flour.
- Small shops buy smaller amounts more often and value reliable delivery days as much as price (Note 2). That is a selling point if we can keep deliveries dependable.
- Demand is expected to rise again in December (Note 2).

**What to leave out**
Note 4 covers cooking oil costs and states that maize flour prices are not affected by them, so it does not bear on this briefing.

**Before this goes further**
1. Resolve the UGX 3,400 versus UGX 3,900 gap by checking the source or sample behind each figure. Note 3's small sample of 12 traders is a reason for caution.
2. Quote the direction (up about 2% to 3%) rather than a single price until then.
3. Check any real decision against real sources, since all four notes are invented.
