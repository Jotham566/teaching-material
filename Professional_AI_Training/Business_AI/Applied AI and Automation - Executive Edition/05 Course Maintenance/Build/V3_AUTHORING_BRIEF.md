## The decision (lecturer, approved 5 November 2026)
The first executive already uses AI tools and asked for the technicals: a touch of the maths behind ChatGPT, a touch of machine learning, foundation models, open weights, fine-tuning, model file formats, prompt engineering, agentic AI. So the course is now 14 sessions of 2 hours (28 hours), benchmarked on the Mathematics for AI and ML course and the machine learning course, WITHOUT coding labs (juniors can do those). Session 0 is delivered and stays. Sessions 1 to 11 teach the technicals with demonstrations the lecturer runs while the learner watches (and may join in). Sessions 12, 13 and 14 are the practicals with the learner, where the documents and templates are created: workflow mapping, governance and buying, data readiness assessment plus roadmap and 90-day plan. The course must also work for financial managers and other executives, for one learner or a group. It is not a law course: spread examples across finance, operations, customer service, HR, sales, compliance and legal. Case company is still Nile Valley Foods Ltd (NVF). No tests, marks or grades. No course price anywhere.

## How every technical concept is taught
Always in this order, one idea per slide cluster: (1) a PICTURE, (2) an everyday ANALOGY (vary the domain: finance, office, shop, hospital, school, court), (3) a CALCULATION with at most three numbers done by hand and shown on the slide, (4) the REAL industry term, (5) ONE demonstration. Write for a lawyer or a finance manager with no maths or computer science: no equations beyond multiply, add and compare; every new word explained at first use; no symbols without a picture. The real names (dot product, Euclidean distance, Manhattan distance, cosine similarity, softmax, gradient) are introduced as pictures first. Say plainly what is simplified. Never state something false to simplify.

## Session shape (default clock, 120 minutes)
10 recall, 35 the ideas (the picture-analogy-calculation-term ladder), 25 demonstration(s) by the lecturer, 5 break, 25 hands-on WITHOUT a computer (a paper or spreadsheet exercise, for example computing distances for three words, scoring a tiny checklist neuron, reading a confusion table; each has a one-page worksheet in the workbook), 15 "what this means for your work" (decision slide: where would this technique help or hurt in the learner's own job), 5 close. Adjust per session but sum to 120. Sessions 12 to 14 are practical: 10 recall, 20 idea, 15 demonstration, 5 break, 55 the learner works, 10 decision, 5 close. The lint wants practice slides (kind `practice`) in sessions 12 to 14 and a `decision` slide in every session. Sessions 1 to 11 should use `practice` slides for the hands-on exercise (label with `label='Hands-on, no computer'`).
Authored slides: 14 to 20 per technical session (lint limit 22). Fields in `SESSION`: see COURSE_BIBLE section 8 and the s00/old s01 files. The `recall` must be about the PREVIOUS new session (Session 1 recalls Session 0: training loop, neuron-free ideas; Session 12 recalls Session 11, etc.). `next` names the next new session title. Session 14 closes the course: `keep_returns` stays True, `next_label='After the course'`, and it needs `one_learner` in facilitator. Add facilitator `demo_steps` (numbered, exact files and commands; mark what is live and what is recorded), `fallback`, `probes` (4 to 6), `misconceptions`, `questions`, `rescue`, `prep`.
Each session ends with a take-away the learner keeps (a one-page sheet). Put it in `keep` and draw it in `keep_visual` (a `card` or `sheet`). Also write the take-away content into `facilitator['takeaway']` (a list of plain lines, ready to paste into a one-page sheet) so the sheet can be generated.

## Visual standard (the lecturer insisted: slides must be visual, with proper illustrations)
At most ONE text-only slide per deck. At least 10 slides with a real, purpose-drawn diagram (kinds below), and every concept slide has a drawn picture: pixel grids with real numbers, neuron diagrams, a drawn decision tree, a scatter of points with the nearest neighbours circled, arrows in a plane for vectors and angles, a city-block versus straight-line grid, bar charts of next-word probabilities, an attention heat map, a stack of layers for a model, boxes for file sizes, a retrieval pipeline, an agent loop with permission badges. Use figures drawn from REAL data where a real run exists (the digits images, the actual probabilities, the actual file sizes). Draw with matplotlib through `figkit.Scene` in your own module `kit/diagrams_tNN.py` (NN = your session number, zero padded; one module per session, it is auto-imported by `kit/diagrams.py`). Register each figure with `@fig('x_<name>')` where `<name>` starts with `tNN_` (for example `x_t04_cosine`); content refers to it with `dict(kind='t04_cosine', ...)`. Text in figures must be at least 14 pt on a slide-size canvas and the Scene audit rejects overlaps and anything off the canvas, so shorten the text or enlarge the drawing. Existing generic kinds you can use: flow, stack, matrix, cycle, bars, timeline, icons, ladder, sheet, card, waffle, bubbles, versus, confusion, roc, descent, network, readiness, roadmap. ## Writing rules (the lint enforces most)
Plain words, short sentences, second person "you"; learner gender unknown (no he or she); slide titles state the point; at most the word limits in `kit/lint_content.py`; body text 18 pt or larger; notes are a first-person spoken script, 45 to 150 words per slide, no stage directions, brackets or "click"; no em or en dashes; no "X, not Y" constructions (rephrase: "A is B." or "Use A when ...", avoid ", not a", "not just", "not only ... but"); banned words (honestly, genuinely, straightforward, dive into, unlock, journey, embark, game-changer, leverage, delve); no exam or marks language; money as "UGX 500,000"; no stock filler openers. Run `python3 kit/lint_content.py N` until 0 problems.
