# Slide QA and formulas brief (7 November 2026)

Two jobs for every session you are assigned.

## Job 1: check every slide, and fix what is wrong
Render your session with `$S/render_one.sh N` (see REVIEW_BRIEF.md for the setup; PNGs at 100 dpi land in `$S/review/NN/`) and LOOK at every single slide image with the Read tool, including the generated ones (cover, outcomes, plan, recall, how today connects, words, keep). Titles render in a serif face in this preview because of a local font quirk: ignore that. Check:
- Illustrations: nothing overlapping or overlaying other text or shapes, nothing clipped at an edge, no text under about 12 pt equivalent, no empty half-slides, labels sit next to what they label, arrows point at the right things, colours consistent (gold = the thing to look at, red = warning or error), the picture shows what the title says, and every number in a picture agrees with the slide text and the recorded runs.
- Content: the example is easy to follow (everyday first, per TERMS_AND_EXAMPLES_BRIEF.md), the standard term is used and explained, the slide builds on the previous one, spoken notes (45 to 150 words) match the slide.
Fix problems in `content/sNN.py` and `kit/diagrams_tNN.py` (figure fixes: shorten text, enlarge or re-lay out the drawing, split a crowded figure), rebuild, and look again at each changed slide.

## Job 2: add the formulas that students will meet elsewhere in the industry
Wherever the session teaches a function, calculation or method that has a standard formula, show the REAL formula on the slide (in the figure, the table or the points), in standard notation, next to the worked numbers from the slide's example, and name every symbol in plain words. Do not replace the plain-words calculation: add the formula beside it. Keep to a few formulas per slide. The brand font (Lato) has the maths glyphs (∂ η Σ √ ≈ × ÷ θ α β − ² ᵀ); inside matplotlib figures you may also use mathtext (for example `r'$w \leftarrow w - \eta \, \partial L / \partial w$'`) if it renders cleanly. Also add the formula to the spoken notes in words (say it aloud as 'w new equals w old minus eta times the gradient'), and to `workbook`/`facilitator['takeaway']` where the take-away sheet lists the method. State what each formula is called in industry.

Formula checklist by session (use the ones your sessions teach; verify every formula and every worked number before putting it on a slide):
- S0: weighted sum z = w1x1 + w2x2 + w3x3 + b (and z = w·x + b); activation functions ReLU(z) = max(0, z) and sigmoid σ(z) = 1/(1 + e^(−z)); loss, mean squared error MSE = (1/n) Σ (yᵢ − ŷᵢ)²; gradient descent update w ← w − η ∂L/∂w (learning rate η); backpropagation as the chain rule ∂L/∂w = ∂L/∂ŷ · ∂ŷ/∂z · ∂z/∂w; accuracy, precision, recall, F1, true positive rate and false positive rate.
- S1: z = Σ wᵢxᵢ + b; output a = f(z) with ReLU and sigmoid; a layer as a = f(Wx + b) (a matrix W with one row per neuron); loss for the house example (squared error (y − ŷ)², MSE); one gradient descent step w ← w − η ∂L/∂w with the one-weight numbers on the slide (compute the gradient: for L = (y − ŷ)², ŷ = wx, ∂L/∂w = −2x(y − ŷ), and show that the slide's step sizes follow from it; if the slide's numbers use a simplified step, state the simplification honestly); the chain rule line for backpropagation; epoch.
- S2: Gini impurity G = 1 − Σ pₖ² (the slide's 0.44 to 0.29 values), entropy H = −Σ pₖ log₂ pₖ and information gain named; k-NN with Euclidean d(a,b) = √Σ (aᵢ − bᵢ)² and Manhattan d(a,b) = Σ |aᵢ − bᵢ|; k-NN regression as the mean of the k neighbours' values; majority vote for classification; min-max scaling x' = (x − min)/(max − min); k-fold cross-validation score as the mean of k scores; regression MSE.
- S3: accuracy = (TP + TN)/(TP + TN + FP + FN); precision = TP/(TP + FP); recall (true positive rate) = TP/(TP + FN); false positive rate = FP/(FP + TN); F1 = 2PR/(P + R) (harmonic mean); the ROC curve plots TPR against FPR over thresholds; AUC as the area under it; cost = FP × (cost of a false alarm) + FN × (cost of a miss).
- S4: bag of words counts; TF-IDF: tf-idf(t, d) = tf(t, d) × log(N / df(t)) with the smoothed form scikit-learn uses (say which one the recorded run used: the demo_runs/s04 scripts show it); Euclidean, Manhattan; dot product a·b = Σ aᵢbᵢ; norm ‖a‖ = √Σ aᵢ²; cosine similarity cos θ = a·b / (‖a‖ ‖b‖) with the film ratings numbers.
- S5: softmax pᵢ = e^(zᵢ) / Σⱼ e^(zⱼ); with temperature pᵢ = e^(zᵢ/T) / Σⱼ e^(zⱼ/T) (check the slide's 70.5, 25.9, 3.5 percent and the temperature 2 values); next-token loss (cross-entropy) L = −log p(correct token) with the 18 percent example (−ln 0.18 ≈ 1.71); the language-model objective as the product or sum of log probabilities of each token given the previous ones, P(x₁…xₙ) = Π P(xₜ | x₁…xₜ₋₁); embedding lookup xₜ = E[tokenₜ] + positionₜ; scaled dot-product attention Attention(Q, K, V) = softmax(QKᵀ / √dₖ) V with the slide's toy numbers explained in these terms (honestly: the toy uses a simplified version; say what is left out); top-k and top-p definitions.
- S6: pretraining objective (the same next-token loss, averaged over a huge corpus), perplexity = exp(average cross-entropy) named; benchmark accuracy = correct / total; mixture of experts as output = Σ gᵢ(x) · Eᵢ(x) over the selected experts with gate weights gᵢ (state simplified).
- S7: memory ≈ parameters × bytes per parameter (FP32 = 4, FP16 = 2, INT8 = 1, 4-bit = 0.5); quantisation: q = round(x / s) + z and the dequantised x̂ = s (q − z) with scale s and zero point z (worked on three numbers); break-even volume = fixed cost / (price per use − variable cost per use) for rent versus host.
- S8: temperature recalled with the S5 formula; accuracy on an evaluation set = correct / total; few-shot as conditioning P(output | instructions, examples, input) in words.
- S9: cosine similarity recalled (S4 formula) with the retrieval scores; top-k retrieval as arg top-k over scores; chunk count = ceil(document length / chunk size) if used; LoRA: W' = W + ΔW with ΔW = B A, B of size d × r and A of size r × k, so r(d + k) trainable numbers instead of d × k (check the 24,576 and 294,912 numbers on the slide against it); the prompt budget: tokens used = prompt + retrieved + answer ≤ context window.
- S10: accuracy against a baseline; expected cost of checking = P(error) × cost of an error; precision of a check if relevant.
- S11: approval time = number of approvals × minutes each (the 3 × 2 = 6 minutes); risk = likelihood × impact; least privilege as a set containment (granted ⊆ needed).
- S12: hours saved = runs per month × minutes saved per run ÷ 60; net benefit = benefits − costs; payback period = set-up cost ÷ net benefit per month; ROI = (net benefit ÷ cost) × 100 percent (check the slide numbers: kettle 20,000 a month saving, 60,000 cost, 3 months, 180,000 first-year net, 300 percent; NVF 159,000 a month, 480,000 set-up, 3.0 months, 1,428,000 12-month net).
- S13: risk score = likelihood × impact (the 4 × 2 = 8 flat-tyre example and the register); total cost of ownership = months × monthly fee + set-up (the 2,400 × 36 = 86,400 example); the 2019 Act is law not a formula (leave).
- S14: completeness = filled values ÷ all values × 100 percent; uniqueness = distinct records ÷ all records; readiness score = sum of area ratings (Green 2, Amber 1, Red 0) out of 12; roadmap hours = sum of action hours; KPI change = (new − old) ÷ old.
