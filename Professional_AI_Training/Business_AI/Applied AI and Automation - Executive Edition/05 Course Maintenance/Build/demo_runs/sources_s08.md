# Sources checked for Session 8 (checked 6 November 2026)

- Anthropic, Prompt engineering overview, https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/overview: before prompt engineering, have a clear definition of success criteria, some ways to test against them empirically, and a first draft prompt. The techniques (clarity, examples, structure, role, thinking, chaining) are in "Prompting best practices" on the same site. Also: not every failing test is best fixed by a prompt; a different model can help latency and cost.
- Anthropic, Extended thinking, https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/extended-thinking: the model can think before it answers; thinking tokens are billed as output tokens; the page says newer models use "adaptive thinking", where the model decides whether and how much to think, with an effort setting. The page marks the older fixed-budget mode as legacy.
- OpenAI, Reasoning best practices, https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/reasoning-best-practices (old address platform.openai.com redirects): reasoning models do well with short, direct prompts; step-by-step ("think step by step") instructions are unnecessary and can sometimes hurt; use delimiters; try with no examples first; state constraints; give a precise end goal and success criteria. The page contrasts the two kinds: fast, well-defined work suits the standard GPT models; planning and ambiguous complex problems suit reasoning models.
- Google, Prompting strategies, https://aistudio.google.com/docs/prompting-strategies (old address ai.google.dev redirects): clear specific instructions, few-shot examples (varied, identical formatting), context, response format, prefixes (for example an opening brace to steer JSON), breaking complex prompts into parts; Gemini thinking models reason internally so you usually do not need to ask for step-by-step reasoning; prompt design is iterative; use code execution for arithmetic.
- Agreement across the three: say the goal and the limits clearly, give the format, give examples where the format matters, test and iterate. They differ in detail on how much to guide the thinking of a reasoning model; the slides say what each vendor says and do not generalise beyond it.
- Not claimed: which product is "a reasoning model" at the time of teaching (names change), prices, benchmark scores.
- Re-verified 6 Nov 2026 at https://aistudio.google.com/docs/prompting-strategies (ai.google.dev redirects): "specific and varied examples"; consistent formatting across examples; too many examples can "overfit the response to the examples"; Gemini 2.5 and 3 thinking models make visible step-by-step requests generally unnecessary; iterate by rephrasing. All Session 8 attributions to Google are supported.
Google prompting strategies (aistudio.google.com/docs/prompting-strategies), checked 6 Nov 2026: varied examples with consistent format; warns that too many examples can cause overfitting to them.
