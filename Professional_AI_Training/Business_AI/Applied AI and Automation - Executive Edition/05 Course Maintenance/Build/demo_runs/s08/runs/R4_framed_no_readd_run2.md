**Results: Net pay = UGX 1,802,898. Total cost to the company = UGX 2,261,250.**

**Working**

1. Hourly rate = 1,800,000 / 160 = 11,250
2. Weekday overtime = 12 h x 11,250 x 1.5 = 12 x 16,875 = 202,500
3. Sunday work = 6 h x 11,250 x 2 = 6 x 22,500 = 135,000
4. Unpaid leave deduction = 1,800,000 / 22 x 1 day = 81,818.18
5. Gross pay = 1,800,000 + 202,500 + 135,000 - 81,818.18 = 2,055,681.82
6. Employee pension (5% of gross) = 102,784.09
7. Employer pension (10% of gross) = 205,568.18
8. Advance repayment = 150,000
9. Net pay = 2,055,681.82 - 102,784.09 - 150,000 = 1,802,897.73, which rounds to **UGX 1,802,898**
10. Total cost = 2,055,681.82 + 205,568.18 = 2,261,250.00, which is **UGX 2,261,250**

**Assumptions and unclear points**

- **Rounding:** I kept full decimals through the calculation and rounded only the final figures. If you round each line to the nearest shilling first (leave 81,818; gross 2,055,682; pension 102,784; employer 205,568), you get the same results: 1,802,898 and 2,261,250.
- **Overtime base:** The overtime and Sunday rates use the 160-hour divisor, while the leave deduction uses 22 days. I applied each as written.
- **Pension base:** I applied pension to gross pay including overtime, as the rules state. Gross is already after the leave deduction.
- **Advance:** The advance is taken after the employee pension contribution. Because it is a flat amount, the order doesn't change net pay. The advance is not part of the company's cost.
- **Not included:** No PAYE (income tax) or NSSF-style statutory deductions are in the rules, so none are applied. A real Ugandan payslip would normally have PAYE, so this is not a full statutory payslip.
