**Renting vs. buying a house**

**Upfront costs**
- Renting: You usually pay a security deposit and the first month's rent, sometimes an agency fee as well. You can move in with little cash.
- Buying: You need a down payment, often 5–20% or more of the price. On top of that come closing costs such as legal fees, stamp duty or transfer taxes, valuation and loan fees. That often adds 2–5% of the price.

**Monthly costs**
- Renting: Rent is predictable, but it can go up when the lease renews.
- Buying: You pay the mortgage (principal and interest), property taxes, insurance, maintenance and sometimes service charges. A fixed-rate mortgage keeps the main payment steady for years. A variable rate can rise.

**Wealth building**
- Renting: None of your rent goes toward ownership. You can still build wealth if you invest the money you would otherwise have spent on a down payment and upkeep.
- Buying: Each principal payment builds equity, and the property may rise in value. Prices can also stay flat or fall, especially over short periods.

**Flexibility**
- Renting: It's easy to move for a job, family or a change of area. You're tied down only for the length of the lease.
- Buying: Selling takes time and costs money (agent fees, taxes). It's a poor fit if you may move within a few years.

**Maintenance and responsibility**
- Renting: The landlord handles most repairs. Your risk is limited.
- Buying: You pay for everything: roof, plumbing, appliances, emergencies. A common rule of thumb is to budget 1–2% of the home's value each year.

**Control and stability**
- Renting: There may be limits on pets, renovations and decorating. The landlord could sell the property or choose not to renew your lease.
- Buying: You can modify the home freely, and you can stay as long as you keep up the payments. Many people value that stability, especially families with children in school.

**Risk**
- Renting: Low financial risk, but you're exposed to rising rents.
- Buying: Your money is concentrated in one asset that can be hard to sell. You face interest-rate risk and could lose the home if you can't pay.

**Renting usually makes more sense when:**
- You expect to move within about 3–5 years
- Your income is uncertain or your savings are thin
- Prices in your area are very high compared with rents
- You prefer flexibility and less responsibility

**Buying usually makes more sense when:**
- You plan to stay roughly 5 years or more
- You have stable income, an emergency fund and the down payment
- Monthly ownership costs are close to local rents
- You want long-term stability and control over your home

**A quick check:** Divide the home price by one year's rent for a similar place (the price-to-rent ratio).
- Below about 15: buying often looks favourable.
- Above about 20: renting often looks better.

Local interest rates, taxes and how long you'll stay can change this a lot. A rent-vs-buy calculator with your own numbers gives a clearer answer. For a major decision, it's worth talking to a qualified financial adviser or mortgage professional.
