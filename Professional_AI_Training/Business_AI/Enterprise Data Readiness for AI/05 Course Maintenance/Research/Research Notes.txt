Compiled 2026-10-29

METHOD AND RELIABILITY (read first)
- Every item was "seen 2026-10-29" via WebSearch result summaries. Fetching primary pages was blocked for almost every host (github.com, eur-lex, iso.org, oxfordinsights.com, newvision.co.ug and others). So nearly all facts are secondary: a search snippet or a third-party page, not the primary document.
- Where a figure rests on one secondary source it is marked "single source". Anything not confirmed is marked UNCONFIRMED. No figures were invented.
- Before any figure goes onto a slide or into a handout, open the primary URL and check the wording. Licence statements especially need a look at each repo's LICENSE file.
- Predictions (Gartner) are forecasts, not measurements. Survey statistics (Cisco, Deloitte, Accenture, S&P) come from vendor or analyst surveys with their own samples.

1.1 Gartner, AI-ready data
- Press release 26 Feb 2025, "Lack of AI-Ready Data Puts AI Projects at Risk" (Q&A with Roxane Edjlali).
- Claim: "through 2026, organizations will abandon 60% of AI projects unsupported by AI-ready data." It is a prediction. The release also cites a Q3 2024 survey of 248 data management leaders: 63% said they lack, or are unsure they have, the right data management practices for AI. It recommends enterprise governance of AI (EGoAI). Gartner's message: AI-ready data needs practices that differ from traditional data management.
- Exact wording came from a search summary and was not checked against the page. The 60% figure is widely repeated with no new evidence behind the repeats.
- URL: https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-02-26-lack-of-ai-ready-data-puts-ai-projects-at-risk (seen 2026-10-29)

1.2 McKinsey, State of AI
- Newest edition reported: "The state of AI in 2026: On the road to ROI", published 25 Aug 2026. Survey 4 May to 8 Jun 2026, 1,719 participants, 97 countries. Reported findings: 89% of organisations use AI; 44% scaling enterprise-wide (38% in 2025); 37% report positive EBIT impact (unchanged from 2025); about 6% attribute 5% or more of EBIT to AI; about one in five say AI operating costs are starting to constrain use.
- These figures come from third-party summaries (kanerika.com, digitalstrategy-ai.com), so check them against McKinsey's page.
- Previous edition: "The state of AI in 2025: Agents, innovation, and transformation", survey 25 Jun to 29 Jul 2025, 1,993 participants, 105 countries. 88% used AI in at least one function; nearly two-thirds had not begun scaling; 23% scaling an agentic system, 39% experimenting; 39% reported enterprise-level EBIT impact.
- Takeaway: adoption is broad and enterprise financial value is concentrated in a small group of high performers.
- URL: https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-state-of-ai (seen 2026-10-29)

1.3 DAMA-DMBOK2 (2017)
- 11 knowledge areas, with Data Governance at the centre of the DAMA wheel: Data Governance; Data Architecture; Data Modeling and Design; Data Storage and Operations; Data Security; Data Integration and Interoperability; Document and Content Management; Reference and Master Data; Data Warehousing and Business Intelligence; Metadata Management; Data Quality. DMBOK2 added Data Integration and Interoperability to the first edition's 10.
- Sogeti's list differs (it includes Data Lifecycle Management); treat as non-standard. A claim that a 2024 revision left the areas unchanged is single source, UNCONFIRMED.
- DAMA maturity: no official DAMA maturity model was found. UNCONFIRMED. DMBOK is a body of knowledge (vocabulary and scope map). Maturity scoring is usually done with DCAM or CMMI DMM.
- URLs: https://www.dataversity.net/data-concepts/what-is-the-data-management-body-of-knowledge-dmbok/ ; https://labs.sogeti.com/data-governance-frameworks-the-dama-dmbok/ (seen 2026-10-29)

1.4 EDM Council DCAM
- DCAM v3 announced 30 Jun 2025; the EDM Council 2026 benchmark report treats v3.1 as current. v3 has 8 components, 34 capabilities, 101 sub-capabilities (v2.2 had 38 capabilities and 136 sub-capabilities). Changes: Business Data Knowledge added as a component; two architecture components merged; Data Control Environment widened to include data management operations; new AI and cloud guidance.
- Scoring: six levels, Not Initiated (1), Conceptual, Developmental, Defined, Achieved, Enhanced (6). One source says five levels; the EDM report and others say six.
- UNCONFIRMED: the full list of all 8 v3 component names.
- Takeaway: a capability scorecard for governing data across the organisation.
- URLs: https://www.projectivegroup.com/dcam-v3-1-the-future-ready-framework-for-strategic-data-management/ ; https://edmcouncil.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/EDMA-Global-Data-Management-Benchmark-Report_2026.pdf (seen 2026-10-29)

1.5 CMMI Data Management Maturity (DMM)
- Launched by CMMI Institute in 2014. ISACA and CMMI merged in 2016, so ISACA owns it. Six categories, 25 process areas: Data Management Strategy; Data Governance; Data Quality; Data Operations; Platform and Architecture; Supporting Processes. Levels 1 to 5: Performed, Managed, Defined, Measured, Optimized (one source says six levels; the standard list is five).
- UNCONFIRMED: current DMM version number (no "DMM 2.0" found). CMMI 2.0 (2018) and 3.0 (2023) are the general model, not the DMM.
- Takeaway: a process-based maturity ladder.
- URLs: https://www.dataversity.net/articles/case-data-management-maturity/ ; https://www.actian.com/what-is-data-management-maturity/ (seen 2026-10-29)

- No formal data maturity model found. Work centres on data monetisation and data liquidity (Wixom and colleagues). Data liquidity is the ease of reusing and recombining data assets. 1.17 GenAI pilot failure statistics, with caveats
a) MIT NANDA, "The GenAI Divide: State of AI in Business 2025" (July 2025)
- Headline: 95% of organisations get zero return despite an estimated 30-40 billion USD enterprise GenAI investment; only about 5% of custom enterprise AI tools reach production.
- Evidence base: review of 300+ public AI initiatives, interviews with 52 organisations, 153 survey responses from senior leaders at four industry conferences. Research ran Jan to Jun 2025. The report is labelled preliminary. Success was defined narrowly (a marked, sustained productivity or P&L gain); the full wording was cut off in the search result.
- Critiques (sourced): small non-random conference sample; self-selected interviewees; six-month window; "no P&L impact", "never reached production" and "failure" are conflated; Fortune inflated the sample size; a working paper judged the statistics descriptive and not inferential.
- Suggested use: "preliminary MIT NANDA research", never "a pilot failure rate". UNCONFIRMED: exact report wording of the 95% sentence.
- URLs: https://mlq.ai/media/quarterly_decks/v0.1_State_of_AI_in_Business_2025_Report.pdf (mirror) ; https://80000hours.org/podcast/episodes/ai-workplace-mit-study/ ; https://www.researchgate.net/publication/395385899_Working_Paper_-_Reassessing_AI_Pilot_Failure_Rates_A_Scoping_Review_and_Managerial_Implications (seen 2026-10-29)

b) Gartner, 29 Jul 2024: "30% of Generative AI Projects Will Be Abandoned After Proof of Concept By End of 2025"
- Causes named: poor data quality, inadequate risk controls, escalating costs, unclear business value. Analyst Rita Sallam. Deployment cost cited as 5-20 million USD. A forecast. UNCONFIRMED: no source found that checks whether the 30% happened.
- URL: https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2024-07-29-gartner-predicts-30-percent-of-generative-ai-projects-will-be-abandoned-after-proof-of-concept-by-end-of-2025 (as cited by Informatica; seen 2026-10-29). Mirror: https://www.hpcwire.com/bigdatawire/this-just-in/gartner-predicts-30-of-generative-ai-projects-will-be-abandoned-after-proof-of-concept-by-end-of-2025/

c) Gartner, 25 Jun 2025: "Over 40% of Agentic AI Projects Will Be Canceled by End of 2027"

Poll: Jan 2025, 3,412 webinar attendees (19% significant investment, 42% conservative, 8% none, 31% waiting or unsure). Gartner says "agent washing" is common, with only about 130 of thousands of agentic vendors real.

2.7 EU AI Act Article 10 (data and data governance)
- Applies to high-risk systems that train models; paragraphs 2 to 5 cover training, validation and testing data. If a system does not train models, they apply to testing data only.
- 10(2): governance and management practices for design choices, annotation, labelling, cleaning, enrichment, aggregation. 10(2)(f) and (g): examine data for bias and take measures to detect, prevent, mitigate. 10(3): data sets relevant, sufficiently representative, as error-free and complete as possible, with appropriate statistical properties. 10(5): exceptional processing of special-category personal data for bias correction, with safeguards.
- URLs: https://ai-act-law.eu/article/10/ ; https://www.artificial-intelligence-act.com/Artificial_Intelligence_Act_Article_10.html (seen 2026-10-29)

2.8 EU AI Act timeline and the Digital Omnibus (status as reported for Oct 2026)
- Commission proposed the Digital Omnibus on AI on 19 Nov 2025. Council position 13 Mar 2026. Political agreement 7 May 2026. Parliament adopted 16 Jun 2026; Council 29 Jun 2026.
- Reported as Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, dated 8 Jul 2026, published in the Official Journal 24 Jul 2026, in force 27 Jul 2026. It amends Regulations 2024/1689, 2018/1139 and 2023/1230.
- Annex III high-risk obligations (Chapter III Sections 1 to 3, which include Article 10) move from 2 Aug 2026 to 2 Dec 2027. Annex I product-embedded high-risk systems move to 2 Aug 2028 (seen in the Dutch EUR-Lex snippet and secondary sources; recheck in the English text). The final text uses fixed dates; the proposed trigger tied to availability of standards was dropped.
- Other changes: Article 50(2) machine-readable marking grace period to 2 Dec 2026 for systems on the market before 2 Aug 2026; new Article 5 prohibitions (non-consensual intimate imagery, child sexual abuse material) from 2 Dec 2026; national sandboxes to 2 Aug 2027; SME relief extended to small mid-caps.
- Chronology and details rest on secondary sources, and the EUR-Lex page was seen as a snippet only.
- URLs: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2026/1744/oj/eng ; https://lawandtechnology.eu/en/digital-omnibus-on-ai-official-journal-regulation-2026-1744/ ; https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/ ; https://www.technology.org/2026/10/13/eu-ai-act-deadlines-after-the-digital-omnibus-what-applies-from-december-2026-to-august-2028/ (seen 2026-10-29)

2.11 Uganda Data Protection and Privacy Act 2019 and Regulations 2021
- Act commenced 3 May 2019; Regulations in effect 12 Mar 2021. The Personal Data Protection Office (PDPO) sits under NITA-U, operationalised Aug 2021.
- Section 19: processing or storage outside Uganda is allowed if the destination offers at least equivalent protection, or the data subject consents. Regulation 30 bars onward transfer to a third country without consent. The "appropriate safeguards" wording is commentary: UNCONFIRMED. DLA Piper reports the PDPO says no per-transfer approval is needed but organisations must keep records of legal basis and safeguards; there is no SCC or adequacy-list mechanism.
- Registration with the PDPO. July 2025: PDPO found Google in breach of section 29 (registration) and section 19, rejecting the argument that section 19 covers only controllers based in Uganda. A Feb 2026 action against WhatsApp/Meta appears in a search summary only: UNCONFIRMED at primary level.
- Breach notification: "immediately" to the PDPO under section 23(1) and Regulation 33, on the prescribed form; no fixed hour limit; the PDPO decides whether data subjects must be told (section 23(3)); registered entities file annual breach reports. Designation of a DPO (section 6) comes from a secondary source: UNCONFIRMED against the statute; no appointment criteria found.
- URLs: https://dataprotection.gi/jurisdictions/uganda/ ; https://globallawexperts.com/international-data-transfers-uganda/ ; https://cipesa.org/2025/07/ugandan-regulator-finds-google-in-breach-of-countrys-data-protection-law-orders-local-registration/ ; https://www.cliffedekkerhofmeyr.com/en/news/publications/2025/Sectors/Technology-Communications/technology-and-communications-alert-13-august-to-register-or-not-to-register-the-ugandan-personal-data-protection-offices-decision-on-the-registration-of-data-controllers ; https://amstelconsulting.ng/blog/uganda-data-breach-notification.html ; https://privacymatters.dlapiper.com/2025/08/uganda-data-protection-regulator-clarifies-compliance-requirements-for-offshore-entities/ (seen 2026-10-29)

6.3 Typical outputs
- UNCONFIRMED as a literature claim: the usual deliverables are a scored profile per dimension, a peer comparison, a gap list and a prioritised roadmap. Cisco's tiering and DCAM's capability scoring are consistent with this, but no source in this pass describes the full deliverable set.

6.4 Failure patterns with a source in this file
- Poor data management practice: 63% of data management leaders lack, or are unsure they have, the right practices for AI (Gartner Q3 2024 survey, n=248; 1.1).
- Data not ready: 61% say their data assets are not ready for GenAI and 70% find scaling with proprietary data hard (Accenture 2024; 1.14). 58% say making data AI-ready is a challenge (Snowflake/ESG; 1.10).
- Data fragmentation: only 19% have fully centralised data infrastructure (Cisco 2025; 1.11).
- Causes of abandonment named: poor data quality, inadequate risk controls, escalating costs, unclear business value (Gartner 2024; 1.17b); cost, data privacy and security risks (S&P; 1.17d).
- Governance gap: 21% have proper governance for agents (Deloitte; 1.13). Unsanctioned tool use is implied by the rise in sanctioned-tool access (under 40% to about 60%) but "shadow AI" prevalence was not measured in any source here: UNCONFIRMED.
- Weak path to production: 5% of custom tools reach production (MIT NANDA, preliminary and contested; 1.17a).
- East Africa specific: regulator registration and transfer rules (2.11 to 2.14), limited in-region cloud and GPU capacity (4.1, 4.2).

6.5 Failure patterns that need a citation before use in teaching
- Data silos, no data ownership, poor metadata, PII in documents, no retrieval evaluation set: these are widely stated in practice, but no source in this pass states them as measured findings. UNCONFIRMED as "literature" claims. The Gartner, Accenture and Cisco figures above support the general point (poor data practice, low centralisation) but not each named pattern.

==========================================================================
SYNTHESIS FOR COURSE DESIGN
==========================================================================
1. Data, governance, infrastructure, talent and strategy recur across Cisco, AWS, Google, Microsoft, Deloitte, DCAM and CMMI DMM. A 12-session programme covering those five plus a security and risk thread matches the market.
2. Maturity ladders have 3, 5 or 6 levels depending on the source. Nobody agrees on a standard scale, so the course should pick one (five levels is the most common) and say why.
3. The sources disagree on how bad failure is: MIT NANDA 95% (preliminary, contested), S&P 42% abandoning most initiatives, Gartner 30% post-POC and 60% abandonment of projects without AI-ready data. Each measures something different, so teach the definitions and not the headline numbers.
4. The "80-90% unstructured" figure has no traceable primary source. Teach it as an analyst estimate and have participants count their own document stores instead.
5. Frameworks from vendors (Microsoft, AWS, Google, Databricks, Snowflake) are platform adoption guides. DAMA, DCAM and CMMI are vendor-neutral but predate generative AI or were only recently extended, so none ties data practice to RAG and semantic search directly.
6. Standards sit apart from frameworks: NIST AI RMF, ISO 42001, ISO/IEC 5259 and EU AI Act Article 10 cover governance and data quality. The EU high-risk dates moved to 2 Dec 2027 (Annex III) and 2 Aug 2028 (Annex I) as reported, so East African firms with EU exposure have more time. Recheck the Official Journal text before teaching dates.
7. East African law is the strongest local gap: four national regimes with different transfer rules (Uganda consent or equivalence, Kenya targeted localisation, Rwanda authorisation, Tanzania permit). No AI-specific rule from Bank of Uganda, IRA or UCC was found, and Uganda's AI strategy is still pending as of the 20 Oct 2026 data strategy launch.
8. In-region infrastructure is thin: Raxio Kampala at 1.5 MW, Tier III capacity short in Uganda, Microsoft-G42 Kenya stalled, Oracle Nairobi not live, and hyperscale regions only in South Africa. Demos should run on a laptop or local server, and the course should teach residency options with real numbers.
9. The free-tool stack is moving fast: MinIO archived or unmaintained, Soda Core and Airbyte source-available, dbt v2 partly proprietary, Amundsen archived, Great Expectations ownership changing. A licence check should be a taught skill and a handout, and demos should name alternatives.
10. Vector search is a commodity (pgvector, Qdrant, Chroma, OpenSearch, FAISS, Milvus are all permissive). The hard parts are parsing, metadata, permission-aware retrieval and an evaluation set, and those are where existing offerings are thinnest.
11. Existing leader courses (MIT, Oxford, Stanford, Google, Microsoft, AWS, DeepLearning.AI) are strategy or product awareness, 99 to 3,850 USD, and none gives a readiness assessment of the participant's own organisation. DCAM and CDMP cover data management without AI retrieval or local law.
12. Genuinely missing in what was found: a vendor-neutral, hands-on programme for mixed executive and technical teams that ends in a scored readiness assessment, uses East African law and infrastructure, and runs on free tools. No such East African offering was found; that is a negative search result.
13. Open verification list before launch: primary reading of Gartner 60% wording, MIT NANDA 95% wording, Oxford Insights ranks for Kenya and Rwanda, ODPC and PDPO current practice, each tool's LICENSE file, and the recall@k and MRR definitions.
