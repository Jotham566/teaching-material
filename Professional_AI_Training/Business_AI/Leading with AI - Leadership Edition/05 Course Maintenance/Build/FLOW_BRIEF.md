## What good flow looks like
1. BRIDGE IN: each slide's notes open with one short sentence linking to what the previous slide just said or showed (its conclusion, its open question, or its number), so the lecturer's voice carries on. Vary the bridges; avoid the same opener twice in a row and avoid mechanical openers such as "Now", "Next" or "Moving on" on every slide. Examples of the kind of thing wanted: "That total of 986 correct sounds impressive, so here is why it can mislead." / "We have the picture; now let me give it numbers." / "You may be wondering what the machine does with those 64 numbers. This slide answers that."
2. PREAMBLE for a new topic or section: when the topic changes, say in one sentence why we are turning to it and what the learner will be able to do with it, before the content.
3. DEMONSTRATIONS: before a demo slide, a lead-in that says what the lecturer is about to do and what to watch for (use the slide's own watch list); after it, a bridge back that asks what they saw and links to the idea it proves. Hands-on and practice slides: say what the learner will do, how long, and that the lecturer walks round; the decision slide: link back to the exercise.
4. HAND-OFF: where natural (about half of the slides), end with a cue that sets up the next slide (an open question, a limit of what was just shown, "let me show you why"). Do not force one on every slide.
5. QUESTIONS keep their pause cue: when a note asks the learner a question, keep it as the last sentence of its paragraph so the Presenter Script pauses there (the builder splits paragraphs after questions). Keep the spoken, first-person, calm expert voice; "I" for the lecturer, "you" for the learner; contractions are fine.
6. The first authored slide must connect from the 'Words we use today' slide (generated), and the last authored slide must lead naturally into the close ('What you keep' slide). `cover_note` should welcome and connect to the previous session (use the session's `recall`, the previous session title) and set the question; `outcome_note` should link the question to the outcomes; `plan_note` to the clock; `words_note` should lead into the first idea slide; `keep_note` should wrap up and point to the next session. Session 1 follows Session 0 (the introduction); Session 14 is the last.

## Hard constraints (the lint enforces most)
Each slide's notes stay 45 to 150 words (cut weaker sentences to make room for bridges; never lose a fact or number; keep recorded numbers exact); session-level notes 25 to 150 words. No stage directions, brackets, "click", "next slide". No em or en dashes. No "X, not Y" constructions (rephrase). Banned words: honestly, genuinely, straightforward, dive into, unlock, journey, embark, game-changer, leverage, delve. No he or she for the learner. No exam or marks language. Never claim an AI result was observed live when it was a recorded run. Paragraph breaks: write notes as a single string; the builder inserts paragraph breaks itself (after questions and about every 48 words), or you may use '\n\n' between paragraphs yourself.

After editing run `python kit/lint_content.py N` for each of your sessions until it reports 0 problems, then `python kit/deck.py N` to confirm the deck builds. Finally re-read the finished script of one whole session in order (from the spec JSON) as if you were reading it aloud, and fix any remaining jumps or repeated openers.
