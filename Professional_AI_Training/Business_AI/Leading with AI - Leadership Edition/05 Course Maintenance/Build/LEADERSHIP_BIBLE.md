# Leading with AI, Leadership Edition: course bible (draft 1, 11 November 2026)

Read this first. It fixes what this edition is, who it is for, the fifteen sessions and how they build on each other, the industry and people threads, the slide pattern and the rules. It is the sister edition of "Applied AI and Automation, Executive Edition" (same build kit, same brand, same 15 x 2 hours), written for leaders who want the business, not the maths.

## 1. Promise and audience
Title: **Leading with AI**. Subtitle: **Where AI pays in your industry, and how to lead your people through it.**
For CEOs, MDs, board members, directors and senior managers in any sector (banking and microfinance, telecom and mobile money, agriculture and agribusiness, manufacturing and FMCG, healthcare and insurance, public sector, then energy, retail and logistics, education, legal and professional services). No maths or technical background needed, and none taught.

The leader leaves able to: (1) spot where AI creates value in their industry and organisation; (2) ask vendors and their own teams the right questions; (3) judge a claim, a pilot and a risk; (4) lead employees through the change; (5) start with a costed, governed 90-day plan and an AI Opportunity Portfolio for their organisation.

## 2. The rule on maths
No formulas, symbols, Greek letters, logs, vectors or algebra, on slides or in notes. Allowed: counts, percentages and shillings, as a leader uses them ("caught 8 of 10 frauds", "2 false alarms for every real fraud", "pays back in 3 months"). Every idea is explained with a picture and an everyday analogy instead. Where the Executive Edition has a formula, this edition has an intuition picture (for example a row of 100 people icons instead of a precision formula). The lint rejects formula symbols.

## 4. The three threads that run through every session
1. **The industry thread.** In Session 0 each leader chooses their industry. Every session from 1 to 10 has an industry tour: one picture with the six main industries and one use of today's idea in each, then one or two named real cases told as a short story (the problem, what the AI does, the result, the catch). Deep cases rotate so each main industry gets two or three deep cases across the course and each secondary industry at least one. Cases come only from `research/` (named, sourced, dated, confidence High or Medium) and are cited on the slide in a small source line.
2. **The people thread.** Every session from 1 to 10 has one "Your people" slide: which roles use it, which tasks change, what skills staff need, and what employees fear or gain. Session 12 brings these together.
3. **The portfolio thread.** The leader's AI Opportunity Portfolio (a one-page register) grows every session: from Session 1 to 10 each hands-on adds one or two candidate uses in the leader's own organisation, written with the session's idea (what goes in, what comes out, who uses it, how we would know it works). Session 11 scores them on value and feasibility, 12 adds the people impact, 13 the risk, 14 the data readiness and the 90-day plan. The final portfolio is the main take-home.

The running worked company stays **Nile Valley Foods Ltd** (fictional FMCG distributor in Jinja), used where a common example is needed; it is always labelled fictional. Real cases are the named organisations.

## 5. Session pattern (120 minutes)
Clock for Sessions 1 to 10: 10 recall and today's question; 40 the idea (picture, everyday analogy, how it works in plain words, what it is good and bad at) and the industry tour with cases; 15 demonstration (live or recorded); 5 break; 25 hands-on on your own organisation (portfolio entries); 15 your people and the leader's decision; 10 close and take-away. Practicals (11 to 14): 10 recall; 20 idea with cases; 10 demonstration or worked example; 5 break; 60 the leader works on their organisation; 10 decision; 5 close.

Slide arc of a Session 1 to 10 deck (about 22 to 28 authored slides):
1. Recall of last session in one picture, then today's leader question (a hook with a real case).
2. The idea, built as a ladder: everyday picture, then what the AI does (input, output), then how it learns or works in plain words with a drawn picture, then what it is good at and bad at. One idea per slide; each slide bridges from the previous.
3. Industry tour: the six-industry picture, then two or three named cases (one slide each, with a result figure and its catch).
4. Demonstration.
5. Your people: roles, tasks, skills, fears.
6. Questions to ask (your team, your vendor).
7. Hands-on: add to your AI Opportunity Portfolio.
8. Decision: what would you do in your organisation?
9. Take-away.

## 6. Writing and visual rules
- Plain words first, then the industry term in bold the first time ("a false alarm, which the industry calls a false positive"). Every term is explained before it is used, or recalled in one line with the session where it was built ("as in Session 3").
- One idea per slide. Each new topic opens with a bridge saying why we turn to it now. Titles, listed alone, read as a story.
- Illustration-rich: every concept slide and every case slide has a purpose-drawn figure (process pictures, icon grids, before and after, maps of who does what, timelines, value and risk grids). Text in figures at least 14 pt. At most one text-only slide per deck.
- Every number on a slide is either from a sourced case (with a source line) or from a recorded run in `demo_runs/`, or is clearly labelled as illustrative. No prices of AI products. No product named as best.
- Uganda and East Africa first in examples, then Africa, then global.
- Spoken notes: first person, 45 to 150 words, flowing from the previous slide.

## 7. Take-homes
Every session: a one-page take-away sheet and new portfolio entries. Learner materials: Course Handbook, Learner Workbook, Take-away Sheets, **Industry Use-Case Atlas** (by industry: uses, named cases, questions), **Questions to Ask** sheet (vendors, IT, board), the AI Opportunity Portfolio template, plus the Executive Edition templates used in the practicals (Business Case Workbook, AI Use Policy, Risk Register, Vendor Question List, Information Inventory, 90-Day Roadmap, Follow-up Kit). The Formula Sheet is not part of this edition.
