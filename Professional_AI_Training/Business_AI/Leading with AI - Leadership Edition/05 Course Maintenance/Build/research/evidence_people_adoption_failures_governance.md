# Evidence file: People, Adoption, Failures, Governance (cross-industry)

Researched 11 November 2026 for "Leading with AI" (Leadership Edition). All sources accessed 11 Nov 2026. Figures are quoted only from pages opened during this research, except where a Caution says a figure comes from secondary coverage.

## Summary

This file holds 44 sourced items in four groups. **PEOPLE (13)**: the strongest evidence comes from controlled studies: customer support (+14% on average, +34% for novices), BCG consultants (large gains inside the "jagged frontier", worse accuracy outside it) and software developers (+26% completed tasks across 4,867 developers). Alongside them sit company claims that were later qualified: Klarna's "700 agents" and its 2025 turn back to human service, IBM's 7,800 roles, and Duolingo's "AI-first" memo. Labour-market forecasts come from the WEF, IMF and ILO, and workplace surveys on shadow AI from Microsoft and BCG. **ADOPTION (9)**: most firms use AI but few have scaled it (McKinsey 2025: 88% use it, about a third are scaling, 39% report any EBIT effect). The MIT NANDA "95%" headline and Gartner's abandonment forecasts belong here. African figures cover generative-AI use per working-age person (Microsoft: Kenya 8.1%, Uganda 6.8% in H2 2025; South Africa 23.1% in Q1 2026), 90+ use cases counted by the GSMA, and African firms' returns (PwC 2026). **FAILURES (7)**: Air Canada, Samsung, Amazon, Zillow, Chevrolet of Watsonville, Deloitte Australia and McDonald's/IBM. **GOVERNANCE (15)**: Uganda's DPPA, the PDPO's ruling against Google and the first criminal conviction under the Act; fines from Kenya's ODPC and Nigeria's NDPC; national AI strategies for Kenya, Rwanda, Nigeria and the AU; and the EU AI Act (as amended by the 2026 Digital Omnibus), ISO/IEC 42001 and the NIST AI RMF. **Uganda's national AI strategy is still a draft. It had not been approved by Cabinet as of 30 Oct 2026** (GOV-04).

Mix: about 13 items are East African (Uganda and Kenya), about 9 are rest-of-Africa or Africa-wide, and about 22 are global benchmarks. The global share is higher than the brief's one-third target. That is because the controlled worker studies and the famous failures are almost all from the US or Europe.

## PEOPLE

### PPL-01 Customer-support agents with a generative AI assistant (NBER study)
- Organisation: an unnamed US-based software firm's customer-support operation (study by Brynjolfsson, Li and Raymond). Country: US (agents in several countries). Industry: customer service. Years: study published 2023, journal version 2025.
- What the AI does: a generative AI assistant suggested replies to agents during live customer chats. Researchers compared agents before and after they got access.
- Result: 5,179 agents. Issues resolved per hour rose **14% on average**, and **34% for novice and low-skilled workers**, with "minimal impact" on experienced, highly skilled workers. AI assistance also improved customer sentiment and was associated with better employee retention (no attrition figure on the abstract page).
- Themes: S5, S8, S12, PEOPLE. People angle: the biggest gains went to the newest, least-skilled staff, which narrowed the skill gap. A lesson for onboarding and training.
- Sources: "Generative AI at Work", NBER Working Paper 31161, https://www.nber.org/papers/w31161 (Apr 2023, rev. Nov 2023). Published as The Quarterly Journal of Economics 140(2): 889-942, 2025, DOI 10.1093/qje/qjae044 (listed on the NBER page).
- Confidence: High (peer-reviewed).
- Caution: this is one firm and one task type (chat support). It shows productivity per agent, not headcount effects.

### PPL-02 BCG consultants and the "jagged technological frontier" (Harvard/BCG experiment)
- Organisation: Boston Consulting Group with Harvard Business School, MIT, Wharton and Warwick researchers. Country: global firm. Industry: professional services. Year: 2023.
- What the AI does: 758 consultants did realistic consulting tasks with or without GPT-4. Some tasks were inside what AI does well and one was deliberately outside it.
- Result: per BCG (21 Sep 2023), on the creative product-innovation task about 90% of participants improved, and the group performed about **40% better** than the control. On a business problem-solving task outside GPT-4's competence, GPT-4 users performed **23% worse** than the control. Lower-baseline performers gained 43% and top performers 17% on the creative task. The HBS AI Institute summary (9 Apr 2026) reports **12.2% more subtasks**, roughly **25% faster**, and about 32% higher quality inside the frontier. Outside the frontier, the control group was correct **84.5%** of the time, against **70.6%** with GPT-4 and **60%** with GPT-4 plus a prompt overview.
- Themes: S3, S5, S8, S10, S12, PEOPLE. People angle: AI lifted weaker performers most, but staff over-trusted it on a task it could not do.
- Sources: "How People Can Create, and Destroy, Value with Generative AI", BCG, https://www.bcg.com/publications/2023/how-people-create-and-destroy-value-with-gen-ai (21 Sep 2023). HBS AI Institute summary, https://aiinstitute.hbs.edu/?p=29980 (9 Apr 2026). Working paper: Dell'Acqua et al., "Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier", HBS Working Paper 24-013 / SSRN 4573321 (Sep 2023).
- Confidence: High.
- Caution: the quality figure differs by source and measure (BCG says 40%, the HBS summary says about 32%). Name the source when quoting. The participants were junior consultants on 2023-era GPT-4.

### PPL-03 GitHub Copilot controlled lab experiment
- Organisation: GitHub / Microsoft Research (Peng, Kalliamvakou, Cihon, Demirer). Country: US. Industry: software. Year: 2023.
- What the AI does: developers were asked to write an HTTP server in JavaScript as fast as possible, with or without the Copilot code-completion assistant.
- Result: the Copilot group "completed the task 55.8% faster" than the control group.
- Themes: S5, S8, S12, PEOPLE.
- Source: "The Impact of AI on Developer Productivity: Evidence from GitHub Copilot", arXiv 2302.06590, https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06590 (13 Feb 2023).
- Confidence: Medium (preprint).
- Caution: this was a single, simple lab task, and the authors included GitHub staff, so it is effectively vendor research. Use PPL-04 for the real-workplace figure.

### PPL-04 Copilot field experiments at Microsoft, Accenture and a Fortune 100 firm
- Organisation: Microsoft, Accenture and an anonymous Fortune 100 company (Cui, Demirer, Jaffe, Musolff, Peng, Salz). Country: US and global. Industry: software and IT services. Years: 2023-2025.
- What the AI does: a random subset of working developers got an AI code-completion assistant during normal work, and their output was compared with colleagues who did not.
- Result: across three experiments and **4,867 developers**, completed tasks rose **26.08%** (standard error 10.3%). Less experienced developers adopted more and gained more.
- Themes: S8, S12, PEOPLE.
- Source: "The Effects of Generative AI on High-Skilled Work: Evidence from Three Field Experiments with Software Developers", Microsoft Research, https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=1148213 (June 2025). Also Management Science, DOI 10.1287/mnsc.2025.00535.
- Confidence: High (randomised, peer-reviewed).
- Caution: authors include Microsoft staff. Each single experiment was noisy, and the 26% is a pooled estimate. It is much smaller than PPL-03's 55.8%, which is a useful contrast in class.

### PPL-05 Klarna: "work of 700 agents", then back to humans
- Organisation: Klarna. Country: Sweden (global). Industry: fintech / payments. Years: 2024-2025.
- What the AI does: an OpenAI-powered assistant answers customer-service chats in many languages. In 2025 the CEO said cost had driven the strategy too far and that quality had suffered.
- Result: Klarna's press release (27 Feb 2024) claimed that in its first month the assistant handled **2.3 million conversations, two-thirds** of service chats, equal to the work of **700 full-time agents**. It claimed resolution in under 2 minutes (down from 11), **25% fewer repeat inquiries**, an estimated **USD 40 million** profit improvement in 2024, 23 markets and 35+ languages. In May 2025 the CEO told Bloomberg the AI-focused approach produced "lower quality" service. Klarna began recruiting remote human agents so customers can always reach a person. Headcount had fallen 22% to about 3,500, mostly through attrition.
- Themes: S3, S5, S10, S12, PEOPLE. People angle: a hiring freeze and attrition rather than layoffs, then partial rehiring. A good case for "cost is not the only metric".
- Sources: Klarna press release, "Klarna AI assistant handles two-thirds of customer service chats in its first month", https://www.klarna.com/international/press/klarna-ai-assistant-handles-two-thirds-of-customer-service-chats-in-its-first-month/ (27 Feb 2024). "Klarna Is Hiring Customer Service Agents After AI Couldn't Cut It on Calls", Entrepreneur (reporting a Bloomberg interview), https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/klarna-ceo-reverses-course-by-hiring-more-humans-not-ai/491396 (9 May 2025).
- Confidence: High that the claims and the reversal were made. The 2024 figures themselves are company claims.
- Caution: the "700 agents" and USD 40m are Klarna's own one-month figures, not audited. The reversal comes from a CEO interview, and Klarna said it still aims to shrink headcount through attrition.

### PPL-06 IBM: back-office roles AI could replace
- Organisation: IBM. Country: US (global). Industry: technology. Year: 2023.
- What the AI does: the CEO said AI and automation could take over routine back-office work such as HR verification letters and internal transfers. He said hiring for such roles would slow or pause.
- Result: CEO Arvind Krishna said he could "easily see 30%" of about **26,000** non-customer-facing roles replaced **over five years**, which implies about **7,800 roles**. IBM said part of the reduction would come from not refilling roles vacated through attrition.
- Themes: S11, S12, PEOPLE.
- Source: "IBM CEO ... pause hiring", Fortune (syndicating a Bloomberg interview), https://fortune.com/2023/05/01/ibm-ceo-ai-artificial-intelligence-back-office-jobs-pause-hiring/ (1 May 2023).
- Confidence: Medium.
- Caution: this is a CEO projection, not a measured outcome or a layoff announcement. Other coverage reported that IBM denied a "blanket" hiring pause.

### PPL-07 Duolingo's "AI-first" memo and walk-back
- Organisation: Duolingo. Country: US. Industry: education technology. Year: 2025.
- What the AI does: the April 2025 memo said the company would gradually stop using contractors for work AI can handle, and add headcount only where teams cannot automate more. After public backlash the CEO said he had not given enough context.
- Result: no published figure on contractor reductions. In August 2025 the CEO told the New York Times the company has never laid off full-time employees and does not plan to.
- Themes: S10 (reputational), PEOPLE. People angle: how an internal AI memo became a public brand problem, and the contractor/employee distinction.
- Source: "Duolingo CEO admits controversial AI memo did not give enough context...", Fortune, https://fortune.com/2025/08/18/duolingo-ceo-admits-controversial-ai-memo-did-not-give-enough-context-insists-company-never-laid-off-full-time-employees/ (18 Aug 2025).
- Confidence: Medium.
- Caution: there is no data on contractor numbers. Teach it as a communication and change-management case, not a jobs statistic.

### PPL-08 WEF Future of Jobs Report 2025
- Organisation: World Economic Forum. Country: global (55 economies). Industry: cross-industry. Year: Jan 2025.
- What it is: a survey of over 1,000 large employers, representing over 14 million workers, on expected job and skill changes to 2030.
- Result: **170 million** jobs created and **92 million** displaced by 2030, a net **78 million**. **39%** of workers' existing skill sets will be transformed or outdated. **85%** of employers plan to prioritise upskilling. **40%** expect to reduce headcount where automation can replace tasks.
- Themes: PEOPLE.
- Source: "170 million jobs to emerge by 2030 but 92 million at risk, WEF warns", People Matters, https://www.peoplematters.in/article/employee-skilling/170-million-jobs-to-emerge-by-2030-but-92-million-at-risk-wef-warns-48552 (article 24 Feb 2026, reporting the Jan 2025 report). Primary: WEF press release, https://www.weforum.org/press/2025/01/future-of-jobs-report-2025-78-million-new-job-opportunities-by-2030-but-urgent-upskilling-needed-to-prepare-workforces/ (Jan 2025). The page refused automated access, so the figures were taken from secondary coverage.
- Confidence: Medium (the figures are consistent across several secondary sources, but the primary page was not read).
- Caution: these are employer expectations, not outcomes. The 170m/92m figures cover all drivers of change, not AI alone.

### PPL-09 IMF: share of jobs exposed to AI
- Organisation: International Monetary Fund (Kristalina Georgieva). Country: global. Year: Jan 2024.
- What it is: the IMF estimated how many jobs AI could affect, by income level.
- Result: almost **40%** of global employment is exposed to AI. The figure is about **60%** in advanced economies, **40%** in emerging markets and **26%** in low-income countries. In advanced economies, roughly half of exposed jobs may benefit and half may see lower labour demand.
- Themes: PEOPLE.
- Source: "AI Will Transform the Global Economy. Let's Make Sure It Benefits Humanity", IMF Blog, https://www.imf.org/en/Blogs/Articles/2024/01/14/ai-will-transform-the-global-economy-lets-make-sure-it-benefits-humanity (14 Jan 2024).
- Confidence: High.
- Caution: "exposed" means affected, not lost. Lower exposure in low-income countries also means smaller productivity gains (see ADO-09).

### PPL-10 ILO: refined global index of generative AI exposure
- Organisation: International Labour Organization (with Poland's NASK). Country: global. Year: May 2025.
- What it is: an occupation-by-occupation estimate of how exposed jobs are to generative AI.
- Result: about **one in four** workers globally is in an occupation with some generative-AI exposure. **3.3%** of global employment is in the highest-exposure category. Exposure is **11%** of employment in low-income countries and **34%** in high-income countries. In the top category, women are more exposed (4.7%) than men (2.4%).
- Themes: PEOPLE. People angle: clerical roles are the most exposed, and they are disproportionately held by women.
- Source: "Generative AI and jobs: A refined global index of occupational exposure", ILO Working Paper 140, https://www.ilo.org/it/publications/generative-ai-and-jobs-refined-global-index-occupational-exposure (20 May 2025).
- Confidence: High.
- Caution: the page gives no Africa-specific figures. Use the low-income-country figure as a proxy and say so.

### PPL-11 Microsoft Work Trend Index 2024: "bring your own AI"
- Organisation: Microsoft and LinkedIn. Country: 31 markets (no African market in the list). Year: May 2024.
- What it is: a survey of about 31,000 knowledge workers on AI use at work.
- Result: **75%** of knowledge workers use generative AI at work. **78%** of AI users bring their own AI tools to work. **52%** are reluctant to admit using it for their most important tasks. **66%** of leaders would not hire someone without AI skills. Only **39%** of users have received AI training from their company.
- Themes: S8, S13, PEOPLE. People angle: shadow AI and the training gap.
- Source: "AI at Work Is Here. Now Comes the Hard Part", Microsoft WorkLab, https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/ai-at-work-is-here-now-comes-the-hard-part (8 May 2024).
- Confidence: High (primary survey).
- Caution: this is a vendor survey, and Microsoft sells AI tools. No African countries were sampled.

### PPL-12 BCG AI at Work 2025: frontline stall and shadow AI
- Organisation: Boston Consulting Group. Country: 11 countries. Year: June 2025.
- What it is: an annual survey of over 10,600 leaders, managers and frontline staff.
- Result: regular AI use among frontline employees has stalled at **51%**. **54%** say they would use AI tools even if not authorised. **41%** worry their job could disappear within ten years. Only **36%** feel adequately trained. Only **25%** of frontline workers say leaders give enough AI guidance.
- Themes: S8, S13, PEOPLE. People angle: middle managers and training drive adoption, and their absence drives shadow use.
- Source: BCG press release, "AI at Work 2025", https://www.bcg.com/press/26june2025-beyond-ai-adoption-full-potential (26 Jun 2025).
- Confidence: High (primary).
- Caution: the 54% applies to all respondents, not only frontline staff. The sample is white-collar workers.

### PPL-13 Microsoft AI skilling pledges in Kenya and Nigeria
- Organisation: Microsoft with Kenya's ICT ministry and Public Service Commission. In Nigeria, with Tech4Dev and Data Science Nigeria. Countries: Kenya, Nigeria. Years: 2024-2027.
- What it is: national programmes to train citizens and public servants in AI and cybersecurity skills.
- Result: Kenya: train **1 million people by 2027** (announced Nov 2024) through the Kenya Skills Hub and Smart Academy. Nigeria: **USD 1 million** over **two years** to train **1 million Nigerians** (announced Feb 2025). No published completion figures.
- Themes: S8, PEOPLE.
- Sources: "Microsoft launches AI skilling initiative in Kenya", ITWeb Africa, https://itweb.africa/content/KBpdgvpmrN97LEew (15 Nov 2024). "Microsoft announces initiative to equip 1 million Nigerians with AI skills", Africa.com, https://africa.com/top-10-news/microsoft-announces-initiative-to-equip-1-million-nigerians-with-ai-skills (21 Feb 2025).
- Confidence: Medium.
- Caution: these are pledges, not outcomes. The Nigeria budget works out to about USD 1 per trainee, which critics noted. Training reach is not the same as workplace adoption.

## ADOPTION

### ADO-01 McKinsey Global Survey: The state of AI in 2025
- Organisation: McKinsey & Company. Country: global (about 2,000 respondents). Year: Nov 2025.
- Result: **88%** of organisations use AI in at least one business function (up from 78%). Nearly **two-thirds** are still experimenting or piloting and have not scaled. **62%** are at least experimenting with AI agents, and **23%** are scaling agents in one or two functions. **39%** report any enterprise-level EBIT impact, most of them under 5% of EBIT. About **6%** are "high performers" (over 5% of EBIT from AI). **32%** expect workforce reductions of 3% or more in the coming year.
- Themes: S11, S12, PEOPLE.
- Source: "McKinsey report shows AI interest but slow scaling", IT Brief UK, https://itbrief.co.uk/story/mckinsey-report-shows-ai-interest-but-slow-scaling (11 Nov 2025). Primary: "The state of AI in 2025: Agents, innovation, and transformation", McKinsey, https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-state-of-ai-2025 (Nov 2025). The primary page timed out, so the figures were taken from secondary coverage.
- Confidence: Medium.
- Caution: these are self-reported survey figures. Check whether a 2026 edition has been published before teaching.

### ADO-02 MIT NANDA, "The GenAI Divide: State of AI in Business 2025"
- Organisation: MIT NANDA initiative. Country: mainly US. Year: July/Aug 2025.
- Result: about **95%** of enterprise generative-AI pilots deliver little or no measurable P&L impact, and about 5% achieve rapid revenue gains. US firms had invested about **USD 35-40 billion**. Buying from specialised vendors and partnering succeeded about **67%** of the time, while internal builds succeeded "one-third as often". More than half of GenAI budgets went to sales and marketing, but the best returns came from back-office automation. The report notes widespread "shadow AI". Firms were tending not to backfill support and administrative roles rather than lay people off.
- Themes: S7, S10, S12, PEOPLE.
- Sources: "MIT report: 95% of generative AI pilots at companies are failing", Fortune, https://fortune.com/2025/08/18/mit-report-95-percent-generative-ai-pilots-at-companies-failing-cfo/ (18 Aug 2025). "Generative AI does nothing for 95% of companies...", The Register, https://www.theregister.com/2025/08/18/generative_ai_zero_return_95_percent (18 Aug 2025).
- Confidence: Medium.
- Caution: this is a preliminary report on a small sample. Outlets describe the method differently (about 150 interviews, 350 employees and 300 deployments in Fortune; 52 interviews, 153 survey responses and 300+ initiatives in The Register). "No measurable P&L impact" does not mean "zero value". Present it as a provocation, not a precise rate.

### ADO-03 Gartner: 30% of generative AI projects abandoned after proof of concept
- Organisation: Gartner. Year: forecast made July 2024 for end-2025.
- Result: "at least 30%" of GenAI projects will be abandoned after proof of concept by the end of 2025. The reasons given are poor data quality, inadequate risk controls, escalating costs and unclear business value. Transformational deployments can cost USD 5-20 million.
- Themes: S10, S12, S14.
- Source: "Gartner: 30% of Gen AI Projects Will Be Abandoned", THE Journal, https://thejournal.com/articles/2024/08/06/gartner-30-of-gen-ai-projects-will-be-abandoned.aspx (6 Aug 2024), reporting the Gartner press release of 29 Jul 2024. The Gartner page refused automated access.
- Confidence: Medium.
- Caution: this was a forecast, not a measured outcome. No later Gartner check of the forecast was found.

### ADO-04 Gartner: over 40% of agentic AI projects will be cancelled by 2027
- Organisation: Gartner (reported by Reuters). Year: June 2025.
- Result: more than **40%** of agentic AI projects will be cancelled by the end of 2027, because of escalating costs, unclear business value or weak risk controls. Of thousands of vendors claiming agentic AI, Gartner estimated only about **130** are real ("agent washing").
- Themes: S6, S10, S11, S12.
- Source: "Over 40% of agentic AI projects will be scrapped by 2027, Gartner says", Reuters via The Star, https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2025/06/25/over-40-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-be-scrapped-by-2027-gartner-says (25 Jun 2025).
- Confidence: Medium (wire report of an analyst forecast).
- Caution: a forecast. Useful for vendor due-diligence discussions.

### ADO-05 Microsoft AI Diffusion (H2 2025): East Africa
- Organisation: Microsoft AI Economy Institute. Countries: Kenya, Uganda, Tanzania, Rwanda. Year: data for H2 2025, reported Jan 2026.
- What it measures: the share of the working-age population using generative AI products, estimated from Microsoft telemetry.
- Result: Kenya **7.8%** (H1 2025) rising to **8.1%** (H2 2025). Uganda **6.8%**, Tanzania **6.8%**, Rwanda **6.3%** (H2 2025). Global average **16.3%**. Adoption in the Global North grew about twice as fast as in the Global South.
- Themes: S8, PEOPLE.
- Source: "Kenya leads AI use in East Africa, but still below global average - Microsoft", Citizen Digital, https://www.citizen.digital/tech/kenya-leads-ai-use-in-east-africa-but-still-below-global-average-microsoft-n375744 (14 Jan 2026). Primary: Microsoft AI Diffusion Report 2025 H2, https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Microsoft-AI-Diffusion-Report-2025-H2.pdf (not opened).
- Confidence: Medium.
- Caution: this counts individuals, not firms. It is a vendor estimate built from telemetry. **This is the only Uganda-specific adoption figure found.**

### ADO-06 Microsoft Global AI Diffusion Q1 2026: Africa
- Organisation: Microsoft AI Economy Institute. Countries: South Africa, Nigeria, Kenya and others. Year: Q1 2026.
- Result: South Africa **23.1%** of the working-age population (up from 21.1% in H2 2025; 46th of 147 economies and highest in Africa).
