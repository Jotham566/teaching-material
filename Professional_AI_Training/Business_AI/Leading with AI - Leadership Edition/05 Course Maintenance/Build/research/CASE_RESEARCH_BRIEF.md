# Case research brief (11 November 2026): named, sourced, real AI cases by industry

## Purpose
"Leading with AI" is a 15-session (2 hours each) course for senior leaders who do not want the maths. They want to know where AI pays in their industry, at work and for their employees. Every session uses real, named organisations as case studies, so the cases must be TRUE, SOURCED and DATED. A wrong figure in front of a bank CEO destroys the course, so accuracy beats quantity.

Market: Uganda and East Africa first, then the rest of Africa, then global leaders. Aim for roughly one third East African, one third rest of Africa or emerging markets, one third global benchmarks, where the evidence exists. Never pad with weak cases.

## The course themes a case can illustrate (tag each case with one or more)
- S1 SEE/READ: AI that sees or reads (images, documents, ID checks, speech)
- S2 PREDICT: prediction from data (credit scoring, churn, fraud, demand forecasting, maintenance, yield)
- S3 MEASURE: judging accuracy claims; the cost of false alarms versus misses
- S4 SEARCH: finding knowledge in documents (semantic search, knowledge bases)
- S5 GENAI: chatbots and generated text or images (customer service, drafting, local languages)
- S6 MODELS: choosing models and vendors (partnerships, local-language models, benchmarks)
- S7 BUY/BUILD: buy, rent or build; data location, cloud, sovereignty, cost
- S8 PROMPTING: everyday staff use of assistants (copilots, productivity)
- S9 KNOWLEDGE: assistants grounded in company documents or customised models
- S10 FAILURE: real failures, incidents, bias, lawsuits, regulator action, reputational damage
- S11 AGENTS: agents and automation that act (with approvals)
- S12 WORKFLOW/ROI: measured process change, time saved, payback, ROI
- S13 GOVERNANCE: policy, regulation, risk, procurement, data protection
- S14 DATA: data readiness, data platforms, digitisation before AI
- PEOPLE: effect on employees, roles, skills, reskilling, change management, adoption

## For every case, record (markdown, one block per case)
- ID (e.g. BNK-01), organisation (named), country, industry, year(s)
- What the AI does, in two plain sentences (no jargon)
- Result: the exact figure(s) as the source states them, with the unit and period. If no measured result is published, write "No published result" (do not estimate).
- Themes (from the list above), and the people angle if any (who uses it, which roles changed)
- Source(s): title, publisher, URL, publication date; accessed 11 Nov 2026. Prefer the organisation itself, regulators, peer-reviewed work, or major outlets (Reuters, FT, Bloomberg, Business Daily, Daily Monitor, The EastAfrican, TechCabal, GSMA, World Bank, IFC, McKinsey, BCG, Harvard Business School cases).
- Confidence: High (primary or two independent credible sources), Medium (one credible secondary source), Low (do not include Low; drop it)
- Caution: anything a teacher must say (vendor claim not independently checked, pilot only, figure from a press release, date of figure)

## Rules
- Use web search and open the pages; quote numbers only from pages you actually read. No figure from memory. Paraphrase; quotes under 15 words.
- Prefer cases with a measured result. Note vendor-claimed figures as such.
- Include failures and mixed results too, not only success stories.
- No prices of AI products. No personal data about private individuals.
- Write your file to the path you are given. Start with a one-paragraph summary and a coverage table (case ID x themes). Then the cases. End with "Gaps": themes or countries where you found nothing reliable.
