# AI case studies: Agriculture and agribusiness; Manufacturing and FMCG

Researched 11 November 2026 for "Leading with AI - Leadership Edition". All pages accessed 11 Nov 2026.

## Summary

This file has 26 cases: 13 in agriculture (AGR) and 13 in manufacturing and fast-moving consumer goods (MFG). Agriculture has good East African evidence: Kenya, Tanzania, Uganda and Burundi. These cases cover crop disease detection, input credit, GenAI advisory chatbots, coffee traceability and document reading in a farmer programme. India and Ghana add the emerging-market view, and John Deere is the global benchmark. Manufacturing and FMCG are weaker in East Africa. The Kenyan and Ugandan breweries (KBL/EABL, Nile Breweries/AB InBev) have published results for digital ordering, but none for AI as such, so they are tagged as data and digitisation cases (S14). The strongest measured AI results in this industry are global: Unilever digital twins, Heidelberg Materials cement kiln AI, the P&G field experiment, Coca-Cola suggested orders and Nestlé's NesGPT. The failures and mixed results are Gro Intelligence (shut down in 2024), Farmer.Chat (one query in four unanswered), PlantVillage Nuru (accuracy depends on use), the Makerere cassava app, GE Predix, Coca-Cola's AI Christmas advert and Unilever's AI video interviews. Most results are company-reported, and every case says so where it applies.

## Part A: Agriculture and agribusiness

### AGR-01: PlantVillage Nuru (cassava disease app)
- **Organisation:** PlantVillage (Penn State) with IITA, CGIAR and national partners. **Country:** Tanzania (fieldwork), Kenya (Busia County evaluation). **Industry:** Agriculture (cassava). **Years:** Field study published December 2020.
- **What the AI does:** A farmer or extension worker points a phone camera at cassava leaves, and the app names the disease and gives advice. It works offline.
- **Result:** The peer-reviewed abstract reports that Nuru was 74 to 88% accurate when it examined six leaves per plant. That was similar to cassava experts and better than extension agents (40 to 58%) and farmers (18 to 31%). Accuracy on a single leaf was much lower: 49% overall in the field, and 21% for cassava brown streak disease. In an image test, the 2020 model scored 65%, against 49% for trained extension officers and 23% for trained farmers.
- **Themes:** S1, S3, S10 (accuracy depends on how the tool is used), PEOPLE (extension agents and lead farmers are the users; it compares the AI with people in different roles).
- **Source:** Mrisho L.M. et al., "Accuracy of a Smartphone-Based Object Detection Model, PlantVillage Nuru, in Identifying the Foliar Symptoms of the Viral Diseases of Cassava - CMD and CBSD", *Frontiers in Plant Science* 11:590889, 18 Dec 2020. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7775399 (accessed 11 Nov 2026).
- **Confidence:** High (peer-reviewed).
- **Caution:** The abstract and the results tables give slightly different figures, so quote the abstract range and say the figures are from 2020. A 2020 Kenyan press article claimed "up to 98%" accuracy. Do not use that figure. This is a good S3 lesson: the headline accuracy depends on the method (six leaves versus one leaf).

### AGR-02: Makerere AI Lab cassava diagnosis (mCROPS)
- **Organisation:** Makerere University AI research team (led by Ernest Mwebaze, now at Sunbird AI), with NaCRRI. **Country:** Uganda. **Industry:** Agriculture (cassava). **Years:** About 2014 to 2019; interview published 2023.
- **What the AI does:** A smartphone app reads a photo of a cassava plant and classifies it as healthy or as having one of the main cassava diseases, with an estimate of severity. Farmers and extension workers also uploaded geotagged photos, so researchers could map where disease was spreading.
- **Result:** No published farm-level result. Mwebaze reports about 400 downloads of the published base model, and that leaf spectral data detected disease about six weeks earlier than images.
- **Themes:** S1, S3, S10 (an early version gave wrong answers when pointed at things that were not plants, so the app had to be rebuilt to answer "undetermined"), S14 (built a large open cassava image dataset), PEOPLE (farmers needed training; the app alone was not enough, so the team added advice, access to experts and farmer-to-farmer contact).
- **Source:** "Interview with Ernest Mwebaze: a machine learning-based app for diagnosing plant diseases", AIhub, 16 Mar 2023. https://aihub.org/2023/03/16/interview-with-ernest-mwebaze-a-machine-learning-based-app-for-diagnosing-plant-diseases/ (accessed 11 Nov 2026).
- **Confidence:** Medium (one credible secondary source, an interview with the lead researcher).
- **Caution:** Research project, not a commercial service. The current status of the app is unknown. Use it for the lessons, not for outcomes.

### AGR-03: Apollo Agriculture (input credit for smallholders)
- **Organisation:** Apollo Agriculture. **Country:** Kenya (later also Zambia). **Industry:** Agri-finance, inputs. **Years:** 2016 onwards; figures from 2021 to 2022.
- **What the AI does:** It uses satellite images of farms and machine learning to decide which smallholders get seed and fertiliser on credit, repaid after harvest. Field agents sign farmers up, and approved farmers receive a voucher code by text message.
- **Result:** About 100,000 farmers were served by the end of 2021, through more than 1,000 retailers and 5,000 agents (TechCrunch, March 2022). In June 2021 the company said it served 60,000 farmers. No repayment or default rate is published in these sources.
- **Themes:** S1 (satellite imagery), S2 (credit scoring), S11 (automated credit decisions), S14 (it lent at first partly to build repayment data), PEOPLE (5,000 field agents; insurance from Pula is bundled with each loan).
- **Sources:** "Kenya-based agritech Apollo raises $40 million in SoftBank-led round...", TechCrunch, 21 Mar 2022. https://techcrunch.com/2022/03/21/kenya-based-agritech-apollo-raises-40-million-in-softbank-led-round-joined-by-chan-zuckerberg-initiative-cdc (accessed 11 Nov 2026). "Kenyan agritech Apollo... $1 million", Techpoint Africa, 22 Jun 2021. https://techpoint.africa/news/kenyan-agritech-apollo-1-million/ (accessed 11 Nov 2026).
- **Confidence:** High for what the system does and how many farmers it reached (two credible outlets). There is no independent outcome data.
- **Caution:** Farmer numbers are company claims, and they come from funding announcements. A GSMA case study exists but could not be opened (access blocked), so its figures are not used here.

### AGR-04: Digital Green Farmer.Chat (GenAI advisory chatbot)
- **Organisation:** Digital Green, with Microsoft Research India. **Country:** Kenya, India, Ethiopia, Nigeria. **Industry:** Agricultural extension. **Years:** 2023 to 2026.
- **What the AI does:** A chatbot answers farmers' and extension workers' questions by text, voice or photo, in local languages. It draws its answers only from a vetted library of farming content (retrieval-augmented generation).
- **Result:** In the 2024 paper, more than 15,000 users had asked more than 300,000 questions. Kenya had the most use: 8,805 users and 225,500 questions. The system answered about 75% of questions. About 80% of answers were rated highly faithful to the source content and 67% highly relevant. The average response took 9 seconds. By 2026 Digital Green reported more than one million farmers using it.
- **Themes:** S3, S4, S5, S6 (GPT-4 and GPT-3.5 used for different tasks), S9, S10 (mixed result: one question in four went unanswered, mostly because the content library had gaps), PEOPLE (extension agents are the main users; women and low-literacy users needed simpler onboarding and voice).
- **Sources:** Singh N. et al., "Farmer.Chat: Scaling AI-Powered Agricultural Services for Smallholder Farmers", arXiv 2409.08916 v2, 8 Oct 2024. https://arxiv.org/html/2409.08916v2 (accessed 11 Nov 2026). Lechner I., "Testing AI advisory services: insights from FarmerChat in India and Kenya", Rural 21, 26 Mar 2026. https://www.rural21.com/english/news/detail/article/testing-ai-advisory-services-insights-from-farmerchat-in-india-and-kenya.html (accessed 11 Nov 2026).
- **Confidence:** High (the developer's own paper plus an independent outlet reporting the IFPRI evaluation).
- **Caution:** The paper's country figures do not add up exactly to its headline totals. The "one million farmers" figure is Digital Green's own. IFPRI's formal evaluation (2025 to 2027) was not yet published at the time of research. Reported problems: data costs, low bandwidth, answers too technical for some users, and early distrust of AI advice.

### AGR-05: Gro Intelligence (failure)
- **Organisation:** Gro Intelligence. **Country:** Founded in Kenya, headquartered in New York. **Industry:** Agricultural data and forecasting. **Years:** 2012 to 2024.
- **What the AI does:** It combined data from governments, weather agencies and markets into AI forecasts of crop yields, climate risk and food supply, which it sold to multinationals and governments.
- **Result (failure):** It shut down in June 2024 after failing to raise new financing. It had raised over $125 million in about ten years, including an $85 million round in 2021, and its valuation reportedly fell from $850 million to under $25 million. The founder-CEO was replaced in February 2024, and 60% of staff were laid off in March 2024. A former employee described a "mismatch between the product and the market": too much custom consulting and not enough repeatable revenue.
- **Themes:** S2, S10, S13 (former staff sued over mass-layoff notice; reported SEC inquiry), S14, PEOPLE (layoffs, missed payroll).
- **Sources:** Onukwue A., "A Kenyan agri-data startup tipped to underpin global food security shuts down", Semafor, 4 Jun 2024. https://www.semafor.com/article/06/04/2024/kenyan-ai-data-gro-intelligence-startup-shuts-down. "Ag insights platform Gro Intelligence is closing down", AgFunderNews, 31 May 2024. https://agfundernews.com/breaking-ag-insights-platform-gro-intelligence-is-closing-down (both accessed 11 Nov 2026).
- **Confidence:** High (two independent outlets).
- **Caution:** The SEC inquiry was reported from unnamed sources, and the SEC would not confirm it. Say "reported", never "found". Do not suggest fraud.

### AGR-06: Dimitra and NACCU (coffee traceability for EU deforestation rules)
- **Organisation:** National Coffee Cooperative Union (NACCU) with Dimitra (agtech vendor). **Country:** Kenya. **Industry:** Coffee cooperatives. **Year:** Agreement signed 26 Sep 2025.
- **What the AI does:** Farmers register their plots on a phone app with GPS mapping, and the platform checks the deforestation risk and produces the compliance report that EU buyers require. It is described as "AI-powered" and works offline in English and Swahili.
- **Result:** No published result. The target is more than one million smallholders. The EU takes over 50% of Kenya's coffee exports.
- **Themes:** S7 (a cooperative union buying a vendor platform), S13 (regulation drives adoption: EUDR), S14 (farm-level data before any AI).
- **Source:** "Kenya's coffee farmers go digital to meet EU deforestation rules", AgNavigator (formerly AgTechNavigator), 14 Oct 2025. https://www.agnavigator.com/Article/2025/10/14/kenyas-coffee-farmers-go-digital-to-meet-eu-deforestation-rules/ (accessed 11 Nov 2026).
- **Confidence:** Medium (one trade outlet).
- **Caution:** This is an announcement only. The article does not say which functions use AI. The EU proposed delaying the regulation, so check its current status before teaching.

### AGR-07: One Acre Fund Burundi (AI reads farm paperwork)
- **Organisation:** One Acre Fund, using the Dataiku platform. **Country:** Burundi. **Industry:** Smallholder farm inputs and finance. **Year:** Published July 2026.
- **What the AI does:** Text-reading software (OCR) reads about 80,000 scanned paper delivery forms a year and checks them against digital survey records. Staff then look only at the entries the system flags in yellow or red.
- **Result:** The company says the work fell from more than 1,000 person-days per cycle to an estimated 300 to 500, a 50 to 70% reduction; the headline claim is "up to 70%". More than 80,000 forms were processed in 2 to 3 weeks. The tool went from prototype to pilot in about two weeks.
- **Themes:** S1 (reading documents), S11 (a human approves every flagged item), S12, S14 (paper forms to data), PEOPLE (about 120 review staff; daily targets and weekly bonuses for high performers).
- **Source:** "One Acre Fund" customer story, Dataiku, 30 Jul 2026. https://www.dataiku.com/stories/detail/one-acre-fund (accessed 11 Nov 2026).
- **Confidence:** Medium (vendor customer story).
- **Caution:** This is a vendor claim, and the reduction is the vendor's own estimate. Other reports describe One Acre Fund models that predict which farmers may not repay. That was not on the page read, so do not cite it from this source.

### AGR-08: Farmerline Darli AI (voice and WhatsApp advisor)
- **Organisation:** Farmerline. **Country:** Ghana (used across Africa and beyond). **Industry:** Agricultural advisory. **Year:** Launched March 2024.
- **What the AI does:** Farmers send a text, a voice note or a photo on WhatsApp, or call a voice line, and get advice on pests, fertiliser, harvest and markets in their own language. It can diagnose crop problems from photos.
- **Result:** Used by 110,000 farmers by October 2024. Available in 27 languages, including Twi, Swahili and Yoruba. Named one of TIME's Best Inventions of 2024.
- **Themes:** S1, S5, S6 (local and low-resource languages).
- **Source:** Wilser J., "Farmerline Darli AI", TIME Best Inventions 2024, 30 Oct 2024. https://tollbit.time.com/collections/best-inventions-2024/7094874/farmerline-darli-ai/ (accessed 11 Nov 2026).
- **Confidence:** Medium.
- **Caution:** The user count is from the company. Later figures ("over 1 million users") come from GSMA, whose page could not be opened, so they are not used here. There is no published yield or income result.

### AGR-09: Sunbird AI "Sunflower" (Ugandan-language model)
- **Organisation:** Sunbird AI, launched with Uganda's Ministry of ICT. **Country:** Uganda. **Industry:** Cross-sector, with agriculture as a stated use. **Year:** Unveiled 11 Oct 2025.
- **What the AI does:** A locally built language model that understands and translates Luganda, Runyankole, Ateso, Acholi, Lugbara and other Ugandan languages. The government expects it to send farmers weather updates and crop advice in their own language.
- **Result:** No published result.
- **Themes:** S5, S6 (a local-language model versus global models), S7 (homegrown, sovereignty).
- **Source:** "Uganda launches an Artificial Intelligence (AI) language model", Ministry of ICT and National Guidance, 15 Oct 2025. https://ict.go.ug/media/news/uganda-launches-an-artificial-intelligence-ai-language-model (accessed 11 Nov 2026).
- **Confidence:** Medium (government source).
- **Caution:** No farmer deployment figures yet. Use it as a local-language and sovereignty discussion case, not as proof of results. Tech outlets claim it outperforms global models in most Ugandan languages; that was not verified here.

### AGR-10: ICRISAT and Microsoft AI sowing app
- **Organisation:** ICRISAT with Microsoft, and the Andhra Pradesh and Karnataka governments. **Country:** India. **Industry:** Smallholder crops. **Years:** 2016 to 2017.
- **What the AI does:** It analysed 30 years of climate data and weather forecasts to choose the best sowing date, then texted farmers advice in Telugu or Kannada. Farmers needed only a basic phone.
- **Result:** The 175 farmers in the 2016 pilot achieved "an average of 30% higher yield per hectare". In the 2017 expansion to more than 3,000 farmers, yield gains ranged from 10% to 30% across crops.
- **Themes:** S2 (prediction from data), S12, S14 (decades of climate records).
- **Source:** "Digital Agriculture: Farmers in India are using AI to increase crop yields", Microsoft News Centre India, 7 Nov 2017. https://news.microsoft.com/en-in/features/ai-agriculture-icrisat-upl-india/ (accessed 11 Nov 2026).
- **Confidence:** Medium (one company source).
- **Caution:** The source is Microsoft's own feature, and the figure is not independently checked. It is a small pilot from 2016 to 2017. The comparison group and method are not described. Present 30% as a pilot figure, never as typical.

### AGR-11: Kisan e-Mitra (Government of India chatbot)
- **Organisation:** Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers' Welfare, built with Wadhwani AI (supported by Google.org) and Bhashini. **Country:** India. **Industry:** Public agriculture services. **Years:** Launched September 2023; figures for 2024.
- **What the AI does:** A voice and text chatbot answers farmers' questions about the PM-KISAN income-support scheme, such as payment status and registration, in 11 languages. It finds the farmer's own record and uses an AI model to word the answer.
- **Result:** About 2.69 million questions answered in 186 days, about 13,000 a day; the footnoted period is 6 March to 5 August 2024. Wadhwani reports this as a 668% increase over the old manual grievance process.
- **Themes:** S4, S5, S6 (open Indian translation models), S9 (grounded in scheme records), S12, S13 (public-sector deployment), PEOPLE (it reduces the workload of block and district officials).
- **Source:** "Empowering millions of Indian farmers with instant AI chatbot support", Wadhwani AI, 6 Nov 2025. https://www.wadhwaniai.org/empowering-millions-of-indian-farmers-with-instant-ai-chatbot-support/ (accessed 11 Nov 2026).
- **Confidence:** Medium (the developer's own publication).
- **Caution:** No accuracy figure is published. The "186 days" and the footnoted dates do not match (about 152 days), so quote "about 2.7 million questions in 2024".

### AGR-12: ITC Krishi Mitra (GenAI farmer copilot)
- **Organisation:** ITC Ltd (ITCMAARS platform) with Microsoft. **Country:** India. **Industry:** Agribusiness (commodity buying, inputs). **Year:** 2024.
- **What the AI does:** A chatbot inside ITC's farmer app answers farmers' voice questions in regional languages about crops, weather and practices. It is built on Microsoft's copilot templates.
- **Result:** No published result.
- **Themes:** S5, S6, S7 (built on a cloud vendor's template rather than from scratch), S9.
- **Source:** "ITC's Krishi Mitra uses Microsoft Copilot to help farmers" (Hindu BusinessLine, republished by ITC), 4 Apr 2024. https://itcportal.com/media-centre/press-report/itcs-krishi-mitra-uses-microsoft-copilot-to-help-farmers.html (accessed 11 Nov 2026).
- **Confidence:** Medium.
- **Caution:** Microsoft materials (not opened) talk of a 300,000-farmer pilot and a 10-million target. Those are targets, not results. Use this to show a large commodity buyer building on a vendor template.

### AGR-13: John Deere See & Spray (global benchmark)
- **Organisation:** John Deere. **Country:** USA (also Australia). **Industry:** Farm machinery and row crops. **Years:** 2024 season.
- **What the AI does:** Cameras on the sprayer boom tell weeds from crop plants as the machine moves, and spray herbicide only on the weeds instead of the whole field.
- **Result:** Deere reports that customers saved 59% of herbicide mix on average in 2024, about 8.3 million gallons across roughly 1 million acres. One Kansas farmer's own figures for 2024: running costs of $3.33 per acre against chemical savings of $2.52 per acre, a net cost of $0.81 per acre.
- **Themes:** S1, S3 (in year one the system could not tell velvetleaf from soybeans), S7 (a per-acre subscription fee that farmers dislike), S10 (mixed: savings did not cover costs on that farm), S11 (the machine acts on its own), S12.
- **Source:** Miller D., "Sense and Act Technology Brings Sharper Precision to Spraying", DTN Progressive Farmer, 25 Apr 2025. https://www.dtnpf.com/agriculture/web/ag/equipment/article/2025/04/25/sense-act-technology-brings-sharper (accessed 11 Nov 2026). The original Deere release, "See & Spray customers see 59% average herbicide savings in 2024" (18 Sep 2024), is cited by several outlets but would not load.
- **Confidence:** High for the 59% being Deere's claim (several outlets report it). There is no independent check of the number itself.
- **Caution:** The 59% is a company-reported average; Deere notes that results vary by weed pressure and crop. The single-farm economics are one operator in one season. This works well in class as "ROI depends on your farm".

## Part B: Manufacturing and fast-moving consumer goods

### MFG-01: Procter & Gamble "Cybernetic Teammate" field experiment
- **Organisation:** Procter & Gamble, with Harvard Business School, Wharton and others. **Country:** USA (global product teams). **Industry:** FMCG. **Year:** Experiment May to July 2024; written up 2025 to 2026.
- **What the AI does:** P&G staff working on real product-development problems (baby care, grooming, oral care) used an internal GPT-4 tool to generate and refine ideas. They were randomly assigned to work alone or in teams, with or without AI.
- **Result:** Individuals with AI matched the quality of two-person teams without AI. Teams with AI were three times more likely than individuals without AI to produce top-10% ideas. Time spent fell by 16% for individuals and 13% for teams. AI users reported more enthusiasm and less anxiety. Less experienced staff with AI performed comparably to experienced colleagues.
- **Themes:** S5, S8, S12, PEOPLE (it broke down silos between R&D and commercial staff, and helped less experienced staff).
- **Source:** Nover S., "When AI joins the team, better ideas surface", HBS Working Knowledge, 17 Oct 2025. https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/when-ai-joins-the-team-better-ideas-surface (accessed 11 Nov 2026). Paper: Dell'Acqua F. et al., "The Cybernetic Teammate", *Organization Science* (online June 2026), https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/orsc.2025.20702.
- **Confidence:** High (a randomised experiment by academic researchers).
- **Caution:** HBS says 791 participants and other summaries say 776. Say "about 780 P&G professionals". It was a one-day task after one hour of training, at one company, so longer-term effects are unknown.

### MFG-02: Coca-Cola AI-generated Christmas advert (mixed or failure)
- **Organisation:** The Coca-Cola Company. **Country:** Global (US and UK reaction). **Industry:** Beverages, marketing. **Year:** November 2024.
- **What the AI does:** Coca-Cola remade its 1995 "Holidays Are Coming" truck advert using generative AI video instead of a traditional film shoot.
- **Result (mixed):** Online reaction was strongly negative. Critics called it "soulless" and saw it as a threat to creative workers. Yet System1, which tests how consumers feel about adverts, scored it 5.9, a top score, in both the US and UK. Coca-Cola stood by the campaign.
- **Themes:** S3 (online anger and measured consumer response disagreed), S5, S10 (damage to its reputation), S13 (brand and disclosure policy), PEOPLE (creative jobs, artists' concerns).
- **Source:** "How Coca-Cola's AI holiday ad went from praise to rage", Adweek, issue of 17 Dec 2024. https://www.adweek.com/creativity/how-coca-colas-ai-holiday-ad-went-from-praise-to-rage/ (accessed 11 Nov 2026). Backlash also reported by Campaign Asia (Nov 2024) and Pretoria News (18 Nov 2024).
- **Confidence:** High.
- **Caution:** No sales effect was published. Good for discussion: "Who decides whether this failed: social media or the test panel?"

### MFG-03: Coca-Cola AI suggested orders for retailers
- **Organisation:** The Coca-Cola Company and bottlers (Coca-Cola Refreshments Malaysia; Latin American bottlers). **Country:** Malaysia, Latin America. **Industry:** Beverages, route to market. **Years:** 2024 to 2026.
- **What the AI does:** When a small shop orders on the bottler's app or WhatsApp, the system suggests which products and how many to buy. It bases this on past orders, season, weather and similar shops. The shopkeeper decides.
- **Result:** On the Q2 2024 earnings call, Coca-Cola said retailers receiving AI recommendations in early pilots were more than 30% more likely to buy the recommended products. In Q1 2024, AI suggested orders reached more than 3 million outlets in Latin America. In Malaysia (January to April 2026), 83% of the shops that joined the campaign adopted the recommendations; no size of sales uplift was disclosed. A three-country demand-prediction pilot showed sales 7 to 8% higher than outlets without it (CIO, reported by Fortune, 2025).
- **Themes:** S2 (demand forecasting), S11 (a suggestion with human approval), S12, PEOPLE (sales reps stay alongside the app).
- **Source:** "Coca-Cola uses AI to guide retailer ordering in Malaysia", AI News (artificialintelligence-news.com), 8 Sep 2026. https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/news/coca-cola-ai-retailer-ordering-malaysia/ (accessed 11 Nov 2026).
- **Confidence:** Medium (one trade outlet summarising company statements).
- **Caution:** All figures are company-reported. "30% more likely to buy" is not "30% more sales". Coca-Cola did not disclose the uplift for Malaysia. Check the Q2 2024 transcript before quoting it on a slide.

### MFG-04: Coca-Cola HBC (image recognition, digital twins, GenAI)
- **Organisation:** Coca-Cola HBC (a bottler covering 29 markets in Europe and Africa). **Industry:** Beverages. **Year:** Interview December 2023.
- **What the AI does:** Sales reps photograph shelves and coolers, and image recognition gives an in-store score of how well the products are displayed. A factory "digital twin" (with Microsoft), a virtual copy of a production line, lets engineers test settings before changing the real line.
- **Result:** About 60 to 70% of sales visits use image recognition. A one-line digital twin prototype reportedly delivered 90% energy savings over 12 months. Image recognition takes 3 to 4 months per market to move from learning to adoption.
- **Themes:** S1, S2, S5 (OpenAI tools for staff), S7 (partnership with Microsoft), S12, S13 (responsible-AI review including legal, privacy and cybersecurity), S14 (data quality is "number one challenge"; older lines need sensors fitted), PEOPLE (sales reps and shop-floor users; adoption takes months).

### MFG-10: GE Predix (failure of an industrial AI platform)

### MFG-11: Heidelberg Materials and Carbon Re (cement kiln AI)
