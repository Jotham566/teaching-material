## The standard every slide must meet
1. EXPLAIN BEFORE USE. Every term, symbol, number and formula on a slide is either explained on that slide or on an earlier slide of this session, or was built in an earlier session (then recall it in one line: "as in Session 4"). This includes the small maths words: proportion or share p, squaring, square root, absolute value, average (mean), Σ (say once per session where first needed: "Σ means add up, one term for each ..."), log₂ (what it measures, in words: how many yes-or-no questions), e and e^x where used, subscripts like aᵢ (explain "i counts the features: a₁ is the first ..."). Never put a symbol on a slide that the notes and slide have not introduced.
2. ONE IDEA PER SLIDE, BUILT AS A LADDER. For every concept that has maths: (a) the question it answers and why we need it now (the motivation, linked to the previous slide); (b) an everyday picture and analogy; (c) the worked example computed step by step on the slide with the real numbers, every intermediate value shown (the reader can redo it with a pen); (d) ONLY THEN the general formula, with every symbol named in plain words next to it, and how the worked example fits the formula; (e) how to read the result (range, what high and low mean, what "better" means); (f) where it is used at work or in the demonstration. A concept that needs (a) to (f) usually needs 2 or 3 slides. Split crowded slides. Example for Gini: slide 1 "How mixed is a group?" (pure group, 50/50 group; the share p; a pure group scores 0); slide 2 worked Gini for the 12 days step by step (p rain = 4/12 = 1/3, p dry = 2/3; square each: 0.11, 0.44; add: 0.56; 1 minus that: 0.44), then the formula G = 1 − Σ pₖ² with p and Σ named; slide 3 the split: Gini of each group, weighted by group size (why weighted), the drop = information gain, and the rule "pick the question with the largest gain"; entropy as a second impurity measure with its worked numbers for the same 12 days (H = −(1/3)log₂(1/3) − (2/3)log₂(2/3) ≈ 0.918) and what it means in words, or drop entropy to a single honest mention in the notes if it cannot be taught properly in the time (state which you chose). Example for cross-validation: first a slide on WHY (overfitting explained properly with a picture: a model that memorises its practice questions; training score versus score on unseen cases; built with real numbers), then the k-fold method, then a worked scoring slide: what a fold score is (the accuracy on that fold: correct ÷ 8, for example 5 of 8 = 0.625), the five fold scores written out, their mean computed, the spread (worst and best), then the formula CV score = (1/k) Σ sᵢ with sᵢ named. Use real recorded fold scores from `demo_runs/` where they exist; if you need a worked set of five fold scores and the run did not record them, either add them to the run record by re-running the script (keep the script and record the output with date and versions) or label the example clearly as an illustration.
3. NO JUMPS BETWEEN TOPICS. The order of slides follows the logic of the session: each new topic opens with a short bridge or a section slide that says why we turn to it and how it links to what came before. Reorder slides if the logic needs it (for example: tree, then overfitting shown on the tree, then how to test honestly (cross-validation), then a new section "a second way to learn from examples: look at the most similar past cases", then distance built from one feature to two, then k-NN, then scaling). Use `statement` slides or the slide titles as signposts if helpful.
4. FORWARD REFERENCES are allowed only as a one-line pointer with a plain meaning ("a closeness score from 0 to 1; Session 4 shows how it is computed"). Never use a later session's formula unexplained.
5. KEEP THE AUDIENCE. Executives (finance, operations, HR, legal, sales), no maths background. Plain words, everyday examples first, then the standard industry term (see TERMS_AND_EXAMPLES_BRIEF.md). The maths is shown properly and slowly; it is not removed. Calm, precise, no hype.
6. TRUTH. Never change a recorded number or fact. Recompute every worked number in Python before it goes on a slide or into notes. If a slide's numbers disagree with `demo_runs/`, fix the slide to the record.
