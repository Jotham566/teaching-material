OCR EXPERIMENT: WHAT IT IS AND WHAT IT IS NOT
ocr_experiment.py renders the twelve passages in passages.txt under four fixed print conditions (A clean, B mild, C moderate, D severe), runs Tesseract on each image and measures word accuracy and whether negation words survive. Results from the run of 26 October 2026 are in results.csv and summary.txt. Images for three passages are in images/. A rerun regenerates everything in a new folder.
The passages are the author's own fictional contract wording. The renderings are synthetic approximations of a clean print-out and of photocopies. They are not scans from a firm's archive, and Tesseract 4.1.1 is an older engine than many commercial services. A modern document-intelligence or multimodal model may behave differently. The experiment shows the shape of the problem: reading is reliable up to a point and then collapses, and degradation produces mostly garbled text and some plausible near-misses ("mot required", "sot apply") rather than a clean reversal of meaning.
To rerun: python3 ocr_experiment.py passages.txt new_folder (needs Pillow, numpy and the tesseract command line tool with the eng language pack). Replace passages.txt with wording from any agreement to test that text.
Reading the results: look at the word accuracy and unusable-passage counts by condition, then open results.csv and read ocr_text beside the source for the C rows. Ask which errors a reader would notice, which a keyword search would miss and which could change a legal meaning.
