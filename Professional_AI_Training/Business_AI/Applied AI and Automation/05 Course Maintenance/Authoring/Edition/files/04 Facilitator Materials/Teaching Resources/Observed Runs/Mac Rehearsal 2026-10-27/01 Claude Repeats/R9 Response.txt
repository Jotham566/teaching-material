Under the amended Section 12.6 (Teaching Adaptation 1), Equator must obtain Victoria's **prior written consent before the change of control takes effect**.

The wholly-owned group exception does not apply because the buyer is unrelated.

**Consequence if Equator fails to obtain consent:** The contract states "Any attempted but non-permitted assignment is void." However, a share sale is not technically an assignment of the agreement—the legal entity (Equator) remains the contracting party while its ownership changes. The amended clause requires consent for change of control but does not explicitly state the consequence for proceeding without it (unlike assignment, which is declared void).

This creates a drafting gap: the consent obligation is clear, but the remedy for breach is not. Victoria may have claims for breach of contract, but whether the agreement itself becomes void or voidable is uncertain from the contract language alone.

**What Victoria should confirm before advising:**
- Whether the transaction is structured as a share sale (change of control) or asset/business sale (assignment)
- Timeline of the proposed transaction
- Whether Equator has formally requested consent
- Ugandan law position on consequences of breaching a change-of-control consent clause
