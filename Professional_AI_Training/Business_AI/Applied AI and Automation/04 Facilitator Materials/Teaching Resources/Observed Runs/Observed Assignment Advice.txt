Advice to Victoria Services Ltd: proposed assignment by Equator (CONTRACT-01 v2)

Yes. Equator needs Victoria's prior written consent before it assigns the agreement to another company.

Why
- Section 12.6 was replaced by Teaching Adaptation 1. Its first sentence says neither party may assign any rights or obligations without the other's prior written consent.
- The standard wording that let either party assign on notice after a merger, reorganization or sale is gone. Equator cannot assign on notice alone.
- The carve-out for a reorganization within a wholly owned group sits in the change-of-control sentence. It excuses only the change-of-control consent, and only if Equator stays responsible. It does not excuse consent to an assignment, so calling the deal an internal reorganisation does not remove Victoria's right.
- An assignment made without consent is void. Under Section 12.2, consent must be in writing and accepted by both parties.
- The clause sets no reasonableness test for refusing. Whether Ugandan law, which governs, adds one should be checked.

What to confirm before we advise
1. The assignee's legal name, its link to Equator, and the ownership chart.
2. Whether the whole agreement, including the Order Form, or only part is to be assigned.
3. Whether a change of control of Equator comes with it, which needs separate consent.
4. Whether Equator will stay liable after the assignment, in writing.
5. Whether the assignee can deliver the hosted service, support, uptime and response commitments, and whether handling of Victoria's data or support-staff access will change (Section 1.5 requires Victoria's approval of named staff).
6. The timing, and that the request will go to Victoria's Notice Address.
