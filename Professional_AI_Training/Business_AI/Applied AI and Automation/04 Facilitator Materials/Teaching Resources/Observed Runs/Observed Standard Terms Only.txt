Short answer: on the Standard Terms alone, probably not. Consent is needed only for an assignment, and the assignment clause carves out reorganizations, which need notice instead. This is provisional because the Cover Pages were not supplied.

Provisions relied on

Section 12.6: "Neither party may assign any rights or obligations under this Agreement without the prior written consent of the other party. However, either party may assign this Agreement upon notice if the assigning party undergoes a merger, change of control, reorganization, or sale of all or substantially all its equity, business, or assets to which this Agreement relates. Any attempted but non-permitted assignment is void."

Section 12.9: "Any notice, request, or approval about the Agreement must be in writing and sent to the Notice Address."

Section 12.2: "Any waiver, modification, or change to the Agreement must be in writing and signed or electronically accepted by each party."

How they apply

- If Equator stays the contracting party and nothing is transferred, Section 12.6 is not engaged. No consent is needed. Nothing in the supplied terms requires notice either.
- If the agreement is assigned as part of the reorganization, no consent is needed, but Equator must give written notice.

Unknown

- Whether the agreement will be transferred, and to whom.
- Whether Equator remains responsible for performance.
- Equator's ownership structure before and after.
- Whether the reorganization is Equator's own and relates to this Agreement.
- Whether the Key Terms or Order Form change Section 12.6.
- The Notice Address.
- The fees. We have not inferred them.
