CHECKED HISTORICAL CASE NOTE
Goal Relief Development Organisation v Uganda Revenue Authority
Civil Appeal 50 of 2023, High Court Commercial Division, 19 August 2024

The court dismissed the appeal and affirmed the Tribunal. On the programme delivery fee, it found that Goal Ireland supplied fundraising services to the appellant, that the appellant imported those services, and that VAT was correctly assessed. This note explains that historical decision. It does not decide Victoria's liability or certify the law applicable to a present transaction.

Question and evidence
The audit period recorded on printed page 1 is January 2017 to December 2019. Ground 5, introduced on page 22, concerns the 10% programme delivery fee retained by Goal Ireland. The appellant's arguments on pages 22–23 disputed the existence of consideration and an imported service. Those arguments are submissions, not the court's conclusion.

Court's reasoning and disposition
On page 23, the court explains a service and importation of a service, then applies that reasoning to Goal Ireland's fundraising abroad and the appellant's use of the grant funds in Uganda. Page 24 concludes that services were imported and that VAT was correctly assessed. The orders on page 24 affirm the Tribunal, dismiss the appeal and award URA costs in both forums. The judgment bears 19 August 2024.

Statute references as the judgment records them
Printed page 3, lines 24–28 cites section 27(2) of the Tax Appeals Tribunal Act, Cap 341, 7th Revised Edition, for an appeal on questions of law. Pages 6 and 10 identify the Income Tax Act as Cap 338, 7th Revised Edition. Page 22 identifies the VAT Act as Cap 344, 7th Revised Edition and discusses section 5(1)(c). These references report what the court cited. A current consolidated Act and the relevant transaction date must be checked separately before giving present advice.

Assessment figures
Page 1's four rows are UGX 51,867,131; 263,533,867; 316,240,641; and 18,867,131. They total UGX 650,508,770. Figures recited in individual grounds of appeal on pages 2–3 must remain separate. Adding them to those four rows double-counts different descriptions of disputed amounts. The checked arithmetic is in Goal Relief Assessment Check.csv.

Source conflicts and remaining questions
The High Court PDF's opening background refers to TAT Application 77 of 2023. The official URA digest and ULII's Tribunal listing identify the underlying matter as Application 77 of 2021. This note preserves that discrepancy and identifies the High Court decision by Civil Appeal 50 of 2023 and its judgment date. It does not silently resolve the inconsistent year.

A limited fresh search of ULII, Judiciary and URA sources located the High Court listing, the Tribunal listing and the official digest. It did not verify a later appellate disposition. This is not a complete citator clearance. URA's current web guidance cites Cap 345, and a Tribunal-hosted Act reproduction explicitly covers 1 July 2018 to 30 June 2021; neither establishes the complete legislation applicable to a 2026 transaction. Current applicability and subsequent treatment remain separate research tasks.
