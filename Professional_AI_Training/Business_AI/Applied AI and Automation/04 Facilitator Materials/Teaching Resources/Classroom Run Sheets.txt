CLASSROOM RUN SHEETS
All twelve sessions run for 120 minutes. The learner already uses AI assistants. I use familiar tasks to test mechanisms, evidence and implementation decisions. Tool setup and routine typing happen before class or while we discuss the work; I do not teach account creation, file upload or basic chat interaction unless help is requested. Sound explanations let us move promptly to the harder case in the Guide, within the same session time. I operate the tools, and we complete the working record together. Optional slides remain outside the core plan.

Compare three firm tasks, then map calculation, classification, retrieval and drafting to their different mechanisms.
050–070 min | Worked demonstration | 12–13, 16
Predict the consent answer, run it, read KT-1 together and mark the unconfirmed transaction trigger.
070–075 min | Break | —
Take a five-minute break.
075–100 min | Guided application and tests | 14–16
Choose one firm task, describe today's process and evidence owner, then write a success test together.
100–110 min | Firm decision and understanding | 17–19
Choose a proposed pilot task and explain who would review its output.
110–120 min | Transfer and recap | —
Change the example to a deadline reminder. Ask for a mechanism, a failure test and a two-sentence explanation to a partner.
Executive application: Choose one familiar firm task and decide whether a chat, a maintained retrieval workflow or ordinary software is sufficient. Name the evidence and reviewer required before a result is used.
Check: A defensible choice links the mechanism to the task and names a source owner, a success test and a hold condition. More AI is not automatically a better design.
