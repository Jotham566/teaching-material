FICTIONAL FACT VARIANTS. KEEP THEM SEPARATE.
The operative text is Section 12.6 (Assignment) as replaced by Teaching Adaptation 1 in the Key Terms of CONTRACT-01 v2. The unmodified Section 12.6 in the Standard Terms is overridden by the Key Terms. Read both and the precedence rule in the "Using the Framework Terms" box.

BASE: Equator proposes an internal group reorganisation. Ownership and continued responsibility are unconfirmed, and nobody has said whether the agreement will move to another entity. Expected: a conditional answer and an evidence request. Consent is not required for a reorganization within a wholly owned group if Provider remains responsible, and neither condition is established. If the agreement were to move to another entity, that would be an assignment needing Victoria’s prior written consent. Ask for the ownership chart, written confirmation of continued responsibility, and a statement of whether any assignment is planned.
A (Session 4): An unrelated buyer acquires control of Equator through a share sale. Stipulate a change of control and that the transaction is outside the wholly owned group. Expected: Provider must obtain Victoria’s prior written consent before the change takes effect. The group exception does not apply. The unmodified Standard Terms would have let Equator assign on notice after a change of control, but the Key Terms change overrides that.
B (Session 8): Equator proposes to assign the agreement to another entity. Expected: the first sentence of Section 12.6 requires Victoria’s prior written consent to any assignment, and an assignment without it is void. The sentence that excuses consent concerns consent to a change of control after a reorganization within a wholly owned group, and does not excuse consent to an assignment. State the facts you still need (who the assignee is, whether Equator stays liable, whether Victoria wishes to consent).
C (extra check): The reorganization is wholly within the group but Equator will not remain responsible for its obligations. Expected: the stated condition fails, so the exception does not apply. If the reorganization produces a change of control, consent is required. If the agreement moves to another entity, consent is required as an assignment.
These are classroom applications of a fictional adaptation, not advice on a real transaction.
