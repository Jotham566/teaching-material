## Self-study clarity (non-negotiable)
Slides are shared as PDFs and learners revise from them WITHOUT the speaker notes. Write full plain sentences. Define each term on the slide where it first appears, say what the picture shows, give units, name every symbol. No pronoun with an unclear noun, no jargon before it is defined. A real example flagged by the lecturer: 'A model cannot learn from a blank' (blank what?). Ask yourself of every sentence: could a bright 17 year old with high school algebra misread this?

## Industry terms always
Use the real term as the name and explain it in simple words beside it, never an invented friendly name (dot product also called inner product; L2 norm or Euclidean norm; cosine similarity; matrix multiplication; identity matrix; inverse; determinant; rank; eigenvalue and eigenvector; singular value decomposition (SVD); principal component analysis (PCA); partial derivative; gradient; Jacobian; chain rule; learning rate; stochastic gradient descent (SGD); Adam; loss function; backpropagation; random variable; probability mass and density function; expectation; variance; standard deviation; covariance; Gaussian (normal) distribution; central limit theorem; Bayes' theorem; likelihood; maximum likelihood estimation (MLE); maximum a posteriori (MAP); entropy; cross-entropy; Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence; softmax; perplexity; embedding; token; logits; temperature; top-p (nucleus) sampling; attention (query, key, value)). Where a symbol has an industry convention use it: x for inputs, y for targets, y-hat for predictions, w or theta for parameters, L for loss, eta or alpha for the learning rate (say which), n for the number of rows, d for dimension, mu and sigma for mean and standard deviation, p(x) for probability.

## What may be said about ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude and other AI (facts discipline)
These are products built on large language models. Their exact internals are not published. You MAY say: language models of this kind are transformers (Vaswani et al. 2017, 'Attention Is All You Need'); the published attention step is softmax(QK^T / sqrt(d_k)) V; they are trained to predict the next token by minimising cross-entropy; text is turned into tokens and embeddings; the vendors' public APIs expose sampling settings such as temperature and top-p (check by name only: OpenAI, Google Gemini and Anthropic APIs all document temperature and top-p, and top-k in some); Adam (Kingma and Ba 2014) is the standard optimiser family in published work; LoRA (Hu et al. 2021) is a published low-rank fine-tuning method; mixed 16-bit precision is common in published training work; open-weight models (for example Llama, Gemma, Mistral) publish enough detail to show the same mathematics. You MUST NOT state parameter counts, layer counts, training data or costs of closed models, nor claim that a specific product uses a specific technique unless its maker has published it. Use phrasing such as 'published research describes', 'models of this kind', 'in open models such as Llama'. Toy numbers (a 4-word vocabulary, 3-dimensional embeddings) are fine when labelled 'illustrative (made-up numbers to show the arithmetic)'. No invented statistics. The slide 'Where this appears in AI' may include a hands-on activity: ask any assistant to explain or compute today's idea, then check its answer against the learner's own numbers (never paste confidential data into an assistant). Do not recommend one product over another.

## The Python lab (content/labs/sNN_nb.py)
`TITLE = 'Short title'` and `build(solution: bool) -> nb.Notebook` (see kit/nb.py and make_notebooks.py). Same rhythm as KLAi's other labs: `nb.step`, `nb.predict`, `nb.read`, `nb.change`, `nb.check_setup()` once near the top, then `check('label', value, expected, tol)` after each answer and `show_checks()` as the last cell. TODO cells: in the learner version (`solution=False`) the answer cell holds `answer = None  # your code here` with a one-line plain-words hint; in the solution version it holds the model answer. Plain-words comments. Use only numpy, pandas and matplotlib (no scikit-learn). Every number printed must equal what the slides state (compute slide numbers in your session file with numpy and reuse them). Expected: 7 to 12 code cells; one tiny real-looking dataset built in the notebook (a small CSV can go in `data/`, generated by a script you also save). Run: `python3 kit/make_notebooks.py N` (writes ../Notebooks and ../Facilitator Materials/Solution Notebooks, runs both, requires the solution to end 'All N checks pass' and the learner version not to).

## The spreadsheet lab (content/labs/sNN_xl.py)
`build(solution: bool) -> xl.XL`; read kit/xl.py fully. The SAME task, data and numbers as the notebook, in a workbook that works in Excel AND Google Sheets (plain functions only: SUM, SUMPRODUCT, MMULT, MINVERSE, MDETERM, TRANSPOSE, SQRT, EXP, LN, LOG, INDEX, IF, ABS, AVERAGE, STDEV.P, VAR.P, COVARIANCE.P, CORREL, NORM.DIST, BINOM.DIST, etc.; array formulas via x.array; no dynamic arrays, LET or LAMBDA). Where a function does not exist in both (eigenvalues, SVD, optimisation), do the idea with cells: a 2 by 2 eigenvalue from the characteristic equation formula, power iteration in rows of cells, gradient descent as a table with one row per step, etc. Gold cells are the learner's; a Check sheet is built for you. Run `python3 kit/check_workbooks.py N`: LibreOffice recalculates the solution version (must say 'All N checks pass') and the learner version (must not). Name the workbook with the title the builder passes to XL (`'Session N: Title'`).

## Facilitator material
Fill `facilitator` completely (prep, misconceptions, probes with good answers, lab_steps naming what to look for in BOTH tracks, rescue, after). Also write per-session `lab_answer_key` in the facilitator dict: a short list of [question, answer] for the PREDICT/READ/CHANGE prompts.

## Notes (spoken script)
Every slide: first-person spoken notes, 45 to 150 words (aim 70 to 120), ending on a question where natural. No stage directions. Matched to what the slide says. House style: no 'X, not Y' constructions, no em or en dashes, no 'honestly', 'genuinely', 'straightforward', 'journey', 'leverage', 'delve'. Address learners as 'you'; no he or she for a learner. Hedge anything legal or uncertain.
